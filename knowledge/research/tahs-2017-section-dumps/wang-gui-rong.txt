   (Author's Note: Mr. Kenjohn Wang was a successful entrepreneur. He was the founder of the
Kenjohn Group, which owned and operated international trade concerns, real estate investment
companies and several hotels in America and Taiwan. The business group is now managed by his
three sons, Kenneth, John and Michael. He also founded the Taiwanese-American Foundation (TAF),
which helped him gain the title of "The Nobel of Taiwan." Kenjohn was born in Taiwan (Formosa)
in 1931, when the island was a part of the Japanese Empire. After graduating from The Junior
College of Administration of Taiwan (now a college of the National Taipei University), he joined the
army, serving as a translator for the (U.S.) Military Assistance Advisory Group (MAAG), while his
future fiancee, Ms. Jacqueline Wang, working at the Taipei American School. In 1972, one year after
President Richard Nixon signed the First Shanghai Communique with Beijing and "the representative
of Chiang Kai-shek" was expelled from the U.N., Kenjohn and his family migrated to Paraguay .
They, however, decided to settle in California the following year. In 1974, Kenjohn bought his first
hotel, Newland Apartment Motel, in Westminster, Orange County. Now, his hotel business empire
includes Ramada Inn (formerly Outrigger Inn), Holiday Inn, and Atrium Hotel, etc. One of the
biggest contributions Kenjohn made during his lifetime was his long and intense involvements in
such areas as local community services, public affairs, charities, scholarships, foundations, chambers
of commerce as well as Taiwanese American affairs and homeland affairs in particular. He set up
the Kenjohn Wang Scholarship at USC. He founded the Taiwan Hotel and Motel Association of
Southern California in 1974 and the Taiwanese Chambers of Commerce of North America in 1987.
He funded and published a commerce-oriented Taiwanese American community newspaper in
1980. He donated his property toaet up LA Taiwan Center in 1988. Acknowledging his services and
contributions, President Ronald Reagan granted him the Decade Award at the White House through
the nomination of the Asia-Pacific American Heritage Committee. In 1989, he was appointed as
a member of the Finance Committee by Ronald H. Brown, the then-Chairman of the Democratic
National Committee. In the same year, Kenjohn moved to Washington, D.C. to take the Office of
President of the Formosan Association for Public Affairs (FAPA), a lobby arm of the Taiwanese
American. Finally, during 2004-2005, Kenjohn was appointed as a member of the President's
Advisory Board by President Chen Shui-bian of Taiwan. Mr. Wang died in Taipei in 2012 at the age
of 81 and left a couple of books he authored, including his memoirs published in 1999.)
