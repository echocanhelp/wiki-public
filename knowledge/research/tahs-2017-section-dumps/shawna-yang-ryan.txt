榮獲 2017 年美國圖書獎（American Book Awards）

    Shawna Yang Ryan was born in Sacramento, California. Her mother and father met in Taichung,
 Taiwan, her mother’s hometown, when her father was stationed there with the US Air Force. A latchkey
 kid of the 1980s, Shawna grew up writing stories to entertain herself after school. In high school, she
 graduated with an International Baccalaureate Certificate and went on to the University of California at
 Berkeley. She initially majored in anthropology, but then switched to English Literature. After graduation,
 she moved to Taichung to study Mandarin. She also taught English at a number of 補 習 班 . While in
 Taiwan that year, she first heard the story of the 228 Massacre, which made a deep impression. She
 returned to the United States in 1999 to attend graduate school in Creative Writing at the University of
 California at Davis. There, she wrote her first novel, Water Ghosts (The Penguin Press 2009) about
 the Chinese American town of Locke, California. In 2002, she returned to Taiwan as a Fulbright Scholar
 and began researching her novel about the 228 Massacre. She continued her Mandarin studies as well.
 After three years in Taipei, she moved back to Berkeley, California. She taught at City College of San
 Francisco for eight years, then moved to Honolulu to teach creative writing at the University of Hawaii at
 Manoa. In 2016, her novel Green Island (Knopf), about the 228 Massacre and Taiwan’s martial law era,
 was published. It received a number of accolades, including the American Book Award. She is currently a
 tenured associate professor and director of the UH Manoa Creative Writing Program.
