Recall the Story Behind the Dissertation

 Writen by Albert S. Lai

 When this dissertation is presented to readers, I feel the need to make some explanations, as it is a paper completed over 50 years ago, and the context of time and space back then is completely different from today. This dissertation, presented in Chinese to the Taiwanese on the island and
 America more than 50 years later, was something I could never have imagined while writing it.
 At the time I wrote this dissertation, the intended readers were only my two thesis advisors, one project eld consultant, and the members of the seminary faculty thesis review committee, totaling no more than 10 people. Among these advisors and committee members, except for Professor Isiah
 Lee（李宗派） (who taught the "Church and Sociology" course at Tainan Theological College and
 Seminary (TTCS)（台南神學院） in 1964), no other professors had been to Taiwan. In the information-scarce 1960s, these professors had very limited understanding of the situation in
 Taiwan and had never heard of the Taiwanese people's desire to "rise up" or the persecution faced by the Presbyterian Church in Taiwan and its members. Therefore, I had to spend a lot of effort in the dissertation, using a signi cant portion to introduce Taiwan and its political ecology.
 The dissertation is a rigid article that seems dull and incomprehensible to general readers today.
 Fifty-three years later, to help today's readers better understand the original intention of writing this dissertation titled "Toward a Community of Hope — A Mission to Formosan Community in Los
 Angeles," I need to narrate the story behind this dissertation to the best of my memory. Only in this way can readers understand and feel the historical context of the establishment of the rst
 Formosan Presbyterian Church in Los Angeles, which is part of the United Presbyterian Church,
 U.S.A. (UPCUSA), which had yet to unify with the Southern Presbyterian Church to become today's "Presbyterian Church (U.S.A.)" (PCUSA).

 The Formation During the Time at
 Tainan Theological College and Seminary

 I enrolled in TTCS in 1959 and graduated in 1967, totaling eight years, including two years of military service and one year of study at Tunghai University. Except for the last two years when Dr.
 Choanseng Song (C.S.Song)（宋泉盛） was the president, the remaining six years were under
 President Shoki Coe（ 彰輝）. President Coe frequently mentioned Barth/Brunner's "Theology of Crisis," Bonhoeffer's "Practical Theology," Cambridge theologian John Oman’s "Religion is
 Secular," and Professor Niebuhr from Union Theological Seminary in New York's "The Believer and the Church as the Conscience of Society," as well as the "Contextual Theology" and

 "Ecumenical Theology" that he himself advocated. I was very familiar with these concepts and deeply in uenced by them. After completing my studies at Tunghai University in 1962, I served as a soldier for two years. During those two years, I witnessed too much of the irrationality, cruelty, murder, deception, and bullying of the Kuomintang（國民黨） government and military......
 Returning to the TTCS after military service in 1964, it was the time when the Presbyterian
 Church in Taiwan was preparing to celebrate the centenary of its mission. The church was making great efforts to complete this task, and it was the last year of signi cant reforms, expansions, and constructions at the TTCS. During this period, the school added departments of social services and mass communication. Several excellent professors joined the faculty, among whom George Todd,
 Dr. Hormel, and Moses Hsu（許牧世） (founder of the Chinese Christian Tribune（基督教論壇
 報）) had the greatest in uence on me. During the celebration of the centenary of Taiwan's mission from 1964 to 1965, many foreign guests and church leaders visited the school, including Dr.
 Van Dusen, president of Union Theological Seminary; Archbishop Desmond Tutu of the South
 African Anglican Church; and other notable gures.
 Many others also came, too numerous to list. These individuals broadened my horizons and deepened my faith foundation. In 1965, during the commemorative activities for the centenary of mission work in Taiwan, President Coe also took on the role of General Assembly Moderator, responsible for the overall planning and arrangement of the celebration activities, which included an unprecedented sports event for Taiwanese Christian Youth (TKC), where thousands of Christian athletes gathered for a large-scale physical competition among brothers and sisters in the faith.
 Additionally, after the morning thanksgiving service on the day of the celebration, a parade was held along the main streets of Tainan City, allowing the citizens to share in the joy of the church's centenary. At the same time, it showcased the unity of the Taiwanese church and its vitality in evangelism to representatives from various countries attending the celebration.
 However, this plan raised suspicions and strong opposition from the Kuomintang, which was already hostile toward the Presbyterian Church, believing that the church aimed to rebel and incite revolution. Due to the Kuomintang's rm opposition and intimidation, President Coe had to agree to separate the TKC youth sports event from the main celebration activities and postpone it by one or two months. The large street parade intended to share joy with the citizens could not be stopped by the Kuomintang, which resorted to intimidation and warnings, strictly prohibiting the use of microphones, loudspeakers, banners, and slogans... At this point, President Coe's relationship with the Kuomintang had become intolerable, and he hurriedly left Taiwan for England after the completion of the celebration activities.
 These events all occurred at the TTCS. President Coe bore these burdens alone, striving to protect the students by keeping them uninvolved and unaware. However, as a senior student and a student council of cer, I could see everything clearly, even though I couldn't participate, and I was lled with righteous indignation. In 1967, after graduating from the TTCS, I decided to leave this country that made me sad and angry.
