**Chao Feng Wu** ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_language "Chinese language"):吳兆峯, born October 19, 1980)[_[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation\_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_], also known as **Ken Wu**, is an American community leader based in [Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles "Los Angeles") area and proponent of [Taiwan Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiwan_Independence "Taiwan Independence") movement.

He is the chapter president of [Formosan Association for Public Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formosan_Association_for_Public_Affairs "Formosan Association for Public Affairs") (FAPA) Los Angeles Chapter[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Wu#cite_note-kuo-1) and _chipo_(branch) head of [World United Formosans for Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_United_Formosans_for_Independence "World United Formosans for Independence") or WUFI, an organization promoting Taiwan independence.[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Wu#cite_note-ettvtra-2) Wu is the secretary-general of Taiwan Center Foundation of Greater Los Angeles,[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Wu#cite_note-3) who also serves as the foundation's board member[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Wu#cite_note-4) and is the chairman of Taiwanese American Heritage Week Committee of Greater Los Angeles for the years 2017 and 2018.[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Wu#cite_note-5)[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Wu#cite_note-6)[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Wu#cite_note-7)

Wu also serves as an executive committee member of the [Democratic Progressive Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Progressive_Party "Democratic Progressive Party") US West Chapter[_[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation\_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_] and due to his many involvements in the overseas Taiwanese community, he also serves as an [Overseas Community Affairs Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overseas_Chinese_Affairs_Council "Overseas Chinese Affairs Council") advisor for the Los Angeles area.

## Early life and education

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Ken_Wu&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Early life and education")]

Wu was born in [Monga District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wanhua_District "Wanhua District") of [Taipei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taipei "Taipei"), [Taiwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiwan "Taiwan") and attended Taipei Dong Yuan Elementary School in [Taipei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taipei "Taipei") until he emigrated to [Seattle, Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seattle,_Washington "Seattle, Washington"), USA at age ten. Wu attended [Eckstein Middle School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eckstein_Middle_School "Eckstein Middle School") and [Roosevelt High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roosevelt_High_School_(Seattle) "Roosevelt High School (Seattle)") in Seattle and finished his high school studies at [Monta Vista High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monta_Vista_High_School "Monta Vista High School") in [Cupertino, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cupertino,_California "Cupertino, California"). He graduated in 2003 from the [University of California, Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Los_Angeles "University of California, Los Angeles"), where he completed his studies in [economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economics "Economics") and [public policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_Policy "Public Policy").[_[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation\_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_]

At UCLA, he directed and produced the stage play _Typhoon Night_ when he served as the director of Taiwanese American Union (TAU)'s Taiwanese Culture Night (TCN).[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Wu#cite_note-8)

Wu decided to pursue a professional career in banking and investments for a major American bank after college before he became more involved in Taiwanese American community works.

Wu believes the [naturally independent"(天然獨)](https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E5%A4%A9%E7%84%B6%E7%8D%A8 "zh-tw:天然獨") generation are more supportive of Taiwan independence and are more willing to sacrifice for their nation for they just want to have a Taiwanese nation for the people of Taiwan. Wu sees the greatest challenge of Taiwan independence movement is its lack of consensus among different generations of Taiwan independence advocates.[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Wu#cite_note-kuo-1) On US-Taiwan relations, Wu suggests Taiwan is not a pawn but a lead role.[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Wu#cite_note-ettvtra-2)

As FAPA Los Angeles chapter president, Wu organized genocide prevention events that aimed to commemorate [February 28 Incident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/February_28_Incident "February 28 Incident") and further mutual understandings of various ethnic groups victimized by government oppression.

Wu in the 2017 event stood with featured keynote speaker [Rebiya Kadeer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rebiya_Kadeer "Rebiya Kadeer") and openly support the independence movement for Taiwan and [East Turkestan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Turkestan "East Turkestan"), and against Chinese government.[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Wu#cite_note-9)

Wu is listed in [Marquis Who's Who](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marquis_Who's_Who "Marquis Who's Who") for "leadership in civic diplomacy and cross-cultural service." Among other accomplishments, Who's Who honored Wu's advocacy for advancing the [Taiwan Travel Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiwan_Travel_Act "Taiwan Travel Act"), a law which permits diplomats from the governments of the U.S. and Taiwan to travel and meet with each other.[[10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Wu#cite_note-10)
