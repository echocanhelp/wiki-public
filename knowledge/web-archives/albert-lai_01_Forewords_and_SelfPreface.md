Half a Century of Faith and Mission
National Taiwanese Presbyterian Council, Presbyterian Church (U.S.A.)

In 1971, Dr. Albert S. Lai（賴信雄） published Toward A Community Of Hope —A Mission
to Formosan Community in Los Angeles, a work both theological and prophetic in nature. At a time
when Taiwanese immigrant churches in North America were still in their infancy—especially the
Formosan Presbyterian Church in Los Angeles (FPCLA), the rst of its kind in the continent—Dr.
Lai offered not merely a historical review, but a spiritual vision. He called the church to transcend
being merely a haven for immigrants and instead become a missional community, a beacon of hope
and a witness to the gospel in the larger society.
A Spiritual Call Amid Cultural Transition
In the early 1970s, most Taiwanese in North America arrived with hopes for education,
professional advancement, or temporary settlement. They faced linguistic barriers, cultural
alienation, and identity uncertainty. Churches quickly became one of the few familiar places in a
foreign land—a spiritual anchor amid transition. Yet Dr. Lai pushed the church to think beyond
comfort. He boldly asserted that if the church is indeed called by God, then it must live as a sent
people—not merely surviving, but witnessing to and transforming its surrounding society.
The Los Angeles Calling: From Ethnic Church to Missional Community
The publication of this book is inextricably linked to the establishment of the Formosan
Presbyterian Church in Los Angeles (FPCLA). As the rst Taiwanese church in North America,
FPCLA did not exist merely to bridge linguistic or cultural gaps. From its inception, it sought to
become a “mission base” in the local community. Dr. Lai documented both the struggles and
aspirations of the early church—how believers found spiritual meaning beyond work and family,
how they supported one another in a foreign land, and how that internal community gave birth to
an external calling to serve and transform.
The Theological Vision of a “Hopeful Community”
The idea of a “hopeful community” carries rich theological weight. It is not a slogan born of
optimism or human willpower, but a lived imagination rooted in biblical truth. Drawing from Acts
and Pauline letters, Dr. Lai described the church not as a safe retreat for insiders, but as a dynamic
body for the hope of the world. A hopeful community is one that chooses love in adversity, chooses

Continuing the Un nished Mission
Over ve decades later, Taiwanese churches in Los Angeles and across North America have
undergone multiple transitions—from immigrant churches to intergenerational, multicultural
communities; from language-based fellowships to agents of justice and public engagement. Yet, the
core challenge of this book remains timeless: Are we still a hopeful community? Are we still bearers
of the gospel light for Los Angeles and beyond? Are we still faithful to the vision rst cast in 1971?
Honoring the Past, Journeying into the Future
We give heartfelt thanks to Dr. Albert Lai for his vision and voice, and we honor the pioneers
who laid the spiritual foundation of FPCLA. Their faith, courage, and servant hearts gave rise not
only to a book or a church, but to a movement that has shaped the spiritual heritage of Taiwanese
Christianity in North America. May this commemorative reprint not merely mark a retrospective
moment, but sound a fresh call to mission—inviting a new generation to rise and respond.
Let us once again embrace the weight of this phrase—Toward A Community Of Hope —A
Mission to Formosan Community in Los Angeles—not merely as a book title, but as a journey still
unfolding.
To God be the glory!

Praise and Recommendation for the Book
"Toward a Community of Hope"
Presbyterian Church in Taiwan

It is with great gratitude that I have the honor of writing the preface for this book. I have been
deeply moved by my readings. The various journeys and historical testimonies left by the author, Dr.
Albert S. Lai（賴信雄）, and the pioneers of faith, both in Taiwan and in the immigrant
environment, are truly commendable and worthy of recommendation to the church and society at
large.
Born in the 1960s, I have experienced the changes and progress of various cultural, political,
and religious environments in Taiwanese society. I resonate with the author’s meticulous
documentation of historical aspects and his incisive analysis, feeling a deep sense of responsibility
and mission towards history.
In the process of globalization and the formation of the concept of a global village, there is
also the effort towards localization, indigenization, and uniqueness; seeking balance between
openness and conservatism; and fostering innovative dialogue amidst competition and
interdependence. Among the many dialogues on various issues, it is rare that the author presents the
concept of "internal integration and external harmony," along with practical suggestions, which is
indeed a profound insight and wisdom.
As Taiwanese Christians, we encourage each other, rmly believing that under the blessings of
our Lord Jesus Christ, we will continue to build a beautiful society together in love, and with
immense hope, we will move towards universality, resiliently moving forward. Let us, guided by the
mission of gospel evangelism and the vision of establishing Taiwan as a new and independent
nation, witness in every era that we are a community moving towards hope.

Rev. En Sheng Pan , 潘恩盛

(NTPC) of the Presbyterian Church (U.S.A.) convenes under the meaningful theme: Preserving and
Passing On the Faith History of NTPC. It calls upon us to re ect anew on the identity and

Let History Ignite a New Flame of Mission

The publication of this book is entirely the result of God's unexpected grace and blessing.
On the afternoon of June 9, 2022, during an initial visit to our esteemed brother, Albert Lai
(hereinafter respectfully referred to as Albert), I chanced upon a doctoral dissertation, sealed in dust
for over half a century. How profound are the riches, wisdom, and knowledge of God, ever
resounding betwixt history and grace!
In the year 1967, Albert graduated from Tainan Theological College and Seminary. The
following year, he journeyed across the seas to pursue further studies at San Francisco Theological
Seminary. For two consecutive summers, he served as an intern at a purely Caucasian Presbyterian
church in Pasadena, Los Angeles. During that time, a group of Taiwanese students and new
immigrants, sojourning in a foreign land, kindled a prayer in their hearts—to worship God in their
mother tongue. Little did they know that this aspiration was precious in the eyes of the Almighty.
Through God’s wondrous guidance, the rst Taiwanese church in Los Angeles was established in
1970. Albert chose this as the subject of his dissertation, completing in 1971 a work of historical
signi cance: Toward a Community of Hope — A Mission to Formosan Community in Los Angeles.
More than fty years later, I had the privilege of reading this dissertation. In that moment, a
fervent desire arose to translate and publish it. Albert humbly inquired, “This is a pastoral research
dissertation; will anyone be interested in reading it?” Yet, it was as if a voice declared, “This history
ought to be seen; this grace must not be forgotten!” Far more than an academic treatise, this work is
akin to a forgotten stream, softly recounting the faith stories of the rst generation of Taiwanese
immigrant Christians. The paradigm of faith left by those early spiritual forebears remains an
undying ame across generations, a most precious gift to the second generation of TaiwaneseAmerican Christians.

In 2016, at the invitation of the Presbyterian Church in Taiwan General Assembly, Evangelism
Committee, I had the honor of serving as a theological lecturer for the Taiwanese Christian Church

forward to the future, deepening my understanding of how Taiwanese people living in different
places seek spiritual belonging through their faith.
