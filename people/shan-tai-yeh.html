Professional record (independent third-party corroboration)

  • Colorado PE license (state register): licensed Professional Engineer, license number PE.0019715, first issue 1982-07-16, last renewed 2021-11-01, status Active, category Architects, Engineers and Land Surveyors; business address of record 9271 East Star Hill Trail, Lone Tree, CO 80124 (per https://opengovco.com/license/75133, Colorado DORA/DPO register mirror) — state-record data, independent of TAH and of the firm site. Note: first-issue 1982 predates the 1979 US arrival year on his self-reported TAH record, so the license start likely reflects practice in Taiwan before emigration (labeled inference).
  • Federal technical manual acknowledgment (US government publication): named on the Technical Working Group that reviewed FHWA Geotechnical Engineering Circular No. 4, “Ground Anchors and Anchored Systems,” Publication No. FHWA-IF-99-015, June 1999 — “Shan-Tai Yeh – Colorado Department of Transportation” (per https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/engineering/geotech/pubs/if99015.pdf, Acknowledgements) — independent federal-agency publication corroborating the CDT affiliation the TAH record claims.
  • ASCE conference paper (peer-reviewed society proceedings): co-author (with Scott A. Anderson and Barry D. Siel) of “Patterned Ground Anchors used for Slope Retention,” Geotechnical Engineering for Transportation Projects, ASCE Proceedings, pp. 2159–2168, published online 2012-04-26 (per https://ascelibrary.org/doi/10.1061/40744%28154%29212) — abstract covers anchor systems up to 200 anchors with individual capacities up to 2000 kN on western-US highway projects.
  • Civil Engineering Magazine (ASCE) article: co-author (with Benjamin P. Arndt and Richard Andrew) of “Making the Grade,” Civil Engineering Magazine Feb 2004, v. 74 n. 2 — western Colorado highway widening on unstable slopes; helicopter-delivered drill rigs, soil nails, ground anchor tiebacks, MSE walls (per https://structurae.net/en/literature/journal-article/making-the-grade, Structurae bibliographic record).
  • Highway Geology Symposium paper: co-author (with Ben Arndt and Richard Andrew, all listed P.E./P.G., Yeh and Associates) of “Landslide Investigation and Mitigation Along US 160 Between Durango and Mancos Colorado using Lightweight Fill, Ground Anchors, and Rockery Buttresses,” 56th Highway Geology Symposium proceedings (per http://www.highwaygeologysymposium.org/wp-content/uploads/56_hgs-OPT.pdf) — same Colorado firm team repeatedly published under his name.
  • Monograph as sole author (library catalog authority record): “Resilient properties of Colorado soils,” Colorado Department of Highways, [1989], 1 online resource (71 pages, illustrations), held as a Colorado State Government Documents eBook — the Marmot Library Network authority record lists Yeh, Shan-Tai as the only author (per https://broomfieldkids.flatironslibrary.org/Author/Home?author=%22Yeh%2C+Shan-Tai%22) — earliest authored work found for him and the only record naming him alone.
  • CDOT research report (co-authored, 1995): co-author with Chunhui Su of “EPS, Flow-Fill, and Structural Fill for Bridge Abutment Highway Backfill. Final Report,” published 1995-08-01 (per https://trid.trb.org/view/448231, TRB/TRID catalog record) — the same report is cited as Yeh, S.T. and Su, C.K. (1995) in the reference list of the CDOT research report CDOT-DTD-R-2000-5 (per http://caee.webhost.utexas.edu/prof/zornberg/pdfs/TR/AbuHejleh_Outcalt_Wang_Zornberg_2000.pdf, p. vi reference list) — his authored research predates the 1999 firm founding the TAH record shows.
  • Named as reviewer on a second state DOT research report: thanked among the concept reviewers of CDOT-DTD-R-2000-5, “Performance of Geosynthetic-Reinforced Walls Supporting the Founders/Meadows Bridge and Approaching Roadway Structures” (June 2000) — the Acknowledgements list Shan-Tai Yeh alongside Hsing Cheng Liu and others (per http://caee.webhost.utexas.edu/prof/zornberg/pdfs/TR/AbuHejleh_Outcalt_Wang_Zornberg_2000.pdf, Acknowledgements p. vi).
  • Third-party of the FHWA affiliation (secondary bibliographic aggregator): a bibliographic listing records the 2012 ASCE Patterned Ground Anchors paper’s authors (Scott A. Anderson, Shan-Tai Yeh, Barry D. Siel) as FHWA-affiliated (per https://doi.org/10.1061/40744(154)212) — this conflicts with the 1999 FHWA circular and the 2000 CDOT report, which both list him under Colorado DOT; kept as dated snapshots, not harmonized (secondary source, aggregator-recorded affiliation).
  • Firm roster on a state DOT approved-consultants list: listed as Shan-Tai Yeh, P.E. — President with contact e-mail styeh@yeh-eng.com for Yeh and Associates, Inc., 2000 Clay Street Suite 200, Denver CO 80211, on the Wyoming DOT Third Party Consultants list, May 2025 (per https://www.dot.state.wy.us/files/live/sites/wydot/files/shared/Utilities/Third%20Party%20Consultants%20May%202025%20(WYO_ES_WORKTYPE_1540752322).pdf) — corroborates the firm’s active multi-state transportation practice and the fixed contact string.
  • Firm practice in an agency engineering newsletter (named project role): the Colorado Geological Survey Newsletter v. 2 n. 4 (October 1999) reports that “Mr. Shan-Tai Yeh of Yeh and Associates and Dr. Hsing-Cheng Liu of CDOT have been working with Roger Pihl, Monica Pavlik, Ty Ortiz, and Ben Arndt of the CGS to provide the geotechnical engineering and geology” for the $200 million State Highway 82 Basalt–Aspen corridor design/construction (per https://coloradogeologicalsurvey.org/wp-content/uploads/woocommerce_uploads/RT-v02n04.pdf) — independent of the firm’s own site.
  • Local newspaper feature, 2003 (China technology-transfer invitation): the Post Independent (Glenwood Springs), 2003-03-24, “Trapani’s bridge expertise goes over well in China” by Lynn Burton, reports that “Trapani, and Yeh & Associates founder Shan-Tai Yeh, were invited to China by a joint venture that includes an Iowa contractor, Iowa State University and the Gansu Province-based Chinese Design Institute,” that the visit covered a proposed ~800-foot Yellow River bridge at Qi Jia Du near Lanzhou, Gansu, and that “With Yeh acting as an interpreter, Trapani addressed a room full of Chinese engineers” (per https://www.postindependent.com/news/trapanis-bridge-expertise-goes-over-well-in-china/) — names him as firm founder and documents the mainland engagement.
  • Civic patronage of a Denver institution: listed as a named individual donor alongside his spouse in the Denver Museum of Nature & Science 2025 Gratitude Report (Mr. Shan-Tai Yeh & Mrs. Lih-Ling Yeh) under the individual donors list (per https://www.dmns.org/media/wd5btwlq/2025-dmns-annual-report-compressed.pdf) — third-party confirmation that the spouse name Lih-Ling on the TAH self-report appears in an institutional donor roll.
