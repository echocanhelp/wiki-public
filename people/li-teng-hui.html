李登輝 (Lee Teng-hui)

Story

李登輝 was born in 1923 in 三芝, a farming town on the northern coast of then-Japanese Taiwan. He served in the imperial army during the war — his brother 李登欽 did not come back, killed at Manila — and after it ran a scholar’s road: 國立臺灣大學, a master’s at Iowa State, a doctorate at 康乃爾大學 in agricultural economics. The call into politics came from 蔣經國, who brought him into the 行政院 in 1972 as the youngest cabinet member in the republic’s history; the arc that followed — 台北市長, 台灣省主席, 副總統 — ended on January 13, 1988, when 蔣經國 died and 李登輝 became the first Taiwanese to hold the presidency. Inside the record lies the list of what came next: the end of 動員戡亂, the apology for 二二八, and in 1996 the first direct presidential election, won in the shadow of missiles in the strait. The diaspora remembered him before and after: in 1983, as 台灣省主席, the Albany-area community threw him a welcome dinner of two hundred at RPI; in 1995, as sitting president at 康乃爾大學, he said the words the community still carries, 「民之所欲，長在我心」; and in 2005 he walked into the U.S. Congress, the first Taiwanese head of state to do so, with more than two thousand greeting him in Los Angeles. When he died on July 30, 2020, the 大洛杉磯台灣會館 kept his memory one more week, hosting his 追思紀念會.

Identity

  • English: Lee Teng-hui (also 輝柏 / Ah-hee in diaspora usage — nickname, not asserted as a formal name)
  • Chinese: 李登輝; Japanese name during the war era: 岩里政男 (改姓名 era, per zh-wiki)
  • Geography / era / roles: 三芝 → 台北 → 美國 (Iowa State, Cornell) → 中華民國總統 (1988–2000); 農業經濟學者; 國民黨主席 (1988–2000)
  • Community tie: proven — 380 corpus mentions across taiwandaily.net-derived pages; named in diaspora records for 1983 Albany, 2005 US visits, 2020 memorials (see record rows).
