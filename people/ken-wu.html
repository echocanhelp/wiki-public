Overview

Chao Feng Wu (Chinese: 吳兆峯; born 19 October 1980), also known as Ken Wu, is an American community leader based in the Los Angeles area and a proponent of the Taiwan independence movement. Public sources describe roles across civic diplomacy, Taiwanese American cultural programming, and overseas Taiwanese political advocacy.

Early life and education

  • Born in Monga (Wanhua) District, Taipei, Taiwan.
  • Attended Taipei Dong Yuan Elementary School until emigrating to Seattle, Washington, USA at age ten.
  • Middle/high school: Eckstein Middle School and Roosevelt High School (Seattle); finished high school at Monta Vista High School in Cupertino, California.
  • Graduated 2003 from the University of California, Los Angeles (UCLA) with studies in economics and public policy.
  • At UCLA, directed and produced the stage play Typhoon Night while serving as director of Taiwanese American Union (TAU) Taiwanese Culture Night (TCN).

Career and community leadership

After college Wu pursued banking and investments at a major American bank, then became more involved in Taiwanese American community work.

Publicly attributed roles and activities (primarily from Wikipedia and linked press; titles and years may need member update for current status):

  • Secretary-general of the Taiwan Center Foundation of Greater Los Angeles and foundation board member (also confirmed on Taiwan Center board materials archived 2026-07-16 as 秘書長).
  • Chapter president, Formosan Association for Public Affairs (FAPA) Los Angeles Chapter.
  • Branch (chipo) head, World United Formosans for Independence (WUFI) — organization promoting Taiwan independence.
  • Chairman, Taiwanese American Heritage Week Committee of Greater Los Angeles (2017 and 2018).

Advocacy themes (public statements summarized from Wikipedia sources)

  • Views the “naturally independent” (天然獨) generation as more supportive of Taiwan independence and more willing to sacrifice for a Taiwanese nation.
  • Sees lack of intergenerational consensus as a major challenge within the independence movement.
  • On US–Taiwan relations, argues Taiwan is not a pawn but a lead role.
  • As FAPA-LA chapter president, organized genocide-prevention events commemorating the February 28 Incident and building mutual understanding among groups victimized by state oppression.
  • At a 2017 event stood with keynote speaker Rebiya Kadeer in support of independence movements for Taiwan and East Turkestan and in opposition to PRC government oppression (per Wikipedia-cited Pacific Times reporting).
  • Marquis Who’s Who listing for leadership in civic diplomacy and cross-cultural service; Who’s Who noted advocacy related to advancing the Taiwan Travel Act (press release cited on Wikipedia, February 2026).
