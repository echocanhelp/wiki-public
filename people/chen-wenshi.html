陳文石 (Chen Wenshi)

Overview

Chen Wenshi is profiled in the 2023 TAHS Publication (台美菁英錄 第二輯). He is a Taiwanese American artist, interior designer, and entrepreneur whose life spans the transition from Japanese colonial rule through the 228 Incident, the rise of Taiwan’s industrial economy, and his eventual emigration to the United States. His career encompasses early struggles as a street vendor and factory worker, rise as a successful interior designer and real estate investor in Taiwan, and later artistic achievements in the United States.

Career

Early Life and Artistic Beginnings

  • Born in 1943 in Sanshing Village, Sanxing Township, Yilan County, Taiwan, during Japanese colonial rule
  • His father worked for the Japanese Mitsui Trading Company and later managed the Pinglin Forestry Company under Japanese rule
  • During the 1945 American bombings of Taiwan, his family took shelter in an air raid shelter; his family’s home was destroyed by napalm
  • In 1947, the 228 Incident occurred during his childhood; his father lost his job when the Japanese colonial administration was replaced by Chinese Nationalist officials
  • His family’s land was forcibly exchanged for worthless agricultural and mining stocks, and their savings were confiscated
  • His father abandoned the family and never returned; Chen grew up without his father, raised by his mother and grandmother
  • His mother was a primary school teacher who supported the family through difficult times
  • As a child, he listened to classical music and read colorful world geography books; he developed a strong interest in drawing and aspired to become a painter
  • His mother encouraged his artistic ambitions despite financial hardship

Early Career and Struggles

  • After elementary school, he attended Taiwan Taichung Commercial High School, walking barefoot to school and binding books with cloth tied around his waist
  • In junior high, he passed the national abacus certification exam and worked at a land bank during summer breaks
  • After high school, he worked in the tax collection office but also studied English at a Catholic church at night to prepare for university
  • In 1960, he was admitted to National Cheng Kung University’s Foreign Language Department, studying English, French, and Japanese
  • During university, he worked as a teaching assistant and taught abacus to support himself
  • After graduation in 1964, he worked in the tax collection office briefly before deciding to pursue art
  • He enrolled in the Hsinsheng Art & Craft School, studying commercial art, but dropped out after one semester due to financial pressures
  • He worked as an apprentice at a craft company, earning 50 NT per month
  • He and his family lived in pig and cow barns by the riverside, losing all their belongings to floods twice

Artistic Development and Success

  • He encountered a painter named Yu Tianxiong who taught him traditional Chinese painting, advertising art, temple crafts, festival cards, paper flowers, lanterns, and kites
  • He began selling his paintings, including Chinese landscapes, at Yangming Road in Taipei, earning approximately 4,000 NT per month
  • He was introduced to Eastern painting (Japanese art) by teacher Chen Ching-hui and later studied sketching under Li Shiqiao
  • At age 22, he re-enrolled at the Hsinsheng Art & Craft School, balancing daytime classes with freelance design work and supporting his girlfriend (future wife) and younger sister
  • He became a successful commercial artist in Taipei, with foreign merchants and celebrities commissioning his work
  • At age 24, he purchased his first house in a Taipei suburb for 40,000 NT
  • At age 27, he bought a second residence on Taipei’s Minsheng East Road for 220,000 NT
  • He established himself as a leading interior designer in Taiwan, training the first generation of professional interior design graduates at Cultural University

Business Ventures and International Experience

  • In the early 1970s, he partnered with Japanese returnee Qiu Wanhong to produce Taiwan’s earliest modular furniture
  • Their company produced high-quality furniture at half the market price, achieving strong sales success
  • He learned business management and cost control from Qiu Wanhong
  • In 1971, he and Qiu invested in stocks through a major stock market crash, losing 2 million NT — a painful lesson in the importance of understanding investments
  • He expanded his business through the Swap Meet market system, eventually becoming a major figure in the industry
  • In 1979, he emigrated to the United States with his family, selling all his Taiwan assets at a 1:46 exchange rate, a decision he later regretted as he missed Taiwan’s economic boom

Life in the United States

  • Upon arriving in Los Angeles, he and his wife purchased a small house in San Gabriel and a five-unit apartment with minimal remaining funds
  • He worked as a painter of houses and buildings, earning the nickname “paint Chen”
  • He was discovered by Zhu Minzhong, a bank manager at the first Chinese bank in LA (Wan Tong Bank), who introduced him to numerous job opportunities
  • He purchased tax-delinquent land through the LA County Treasury Department, earning enough to invest in real estate
  • He studied hotel management at Pomona University and obtained a real estate license
  • In 1984, he built his first 26-unit motel at the corner of San Gabriel Boulevard and the 10 Freeway, securing a loan through Zhu Minzhong’s guarantee at Wan Tong Bank
  • He developed multiple motel and commercial properties, including 68-unit and 58-unit motels
  • In 1988, he shifted focus to pure investment, beginning world travel with his wife by cruise ship
  • He invested in Thailand’s stock market, purchasing shares in BBL Bank when the Thai stock index was at 660, achieving significant returns
  • In 1989, he visited Shanghai but decided against investing due to political risks
  • In 1991, he held his first art exhibition in Los Angeles with the theme “Nostalgia for Home,” featuring rural Taiwanese scenes that resonated deeply with fellow Taiwanese

Legacy

Cultural Advocacy and Community Building

  • He established the North American Taiwanese Artists Association in 1991, the largest overseas Taiwanese art organization
  • He organized the “North American Taiwanese Artists Joint Exhibition” to introduce Taiwanese-American artists to the broader community
  • He donated 8,000 NT from his first art exhibition proceeds to the Taiwan United Foundation for cultural arts activities
  • He supported the “Full Vision” movement to break through KMT media monopolies
  • He co-invested in the “Chunfeng Publishing House” to publish Taiwanese cultural literature
  • He participated in Taiwan’s democratic movement and was involved in overseas Taiwan democratic reform activities
  • He held numerous exhibitions across the United States and internationally, including shows in New York, San Francisco, Paris, and Tokyo

Artistic Philosophy

  • He believes that art should be a personal expression, not merely commercial work — he refused to accept commissioned copies of his paintings
  • His artwork reflects his deep connection to Taiwanese rural life and his nostalgia for his homeland
  • He views his life journey as embodying the resilience of the Taiwanese people through decades of political and social change
  • His artistic career demonstrates the transition from survival-focused labor to creative fulfillment, enabled by the freedoms of life in the United States
