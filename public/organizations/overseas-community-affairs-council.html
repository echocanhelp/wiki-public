Overseas Community Affairs Council (僑務委員會)

The Overseas Community Affairs Council (OCAC; Chinese: 僑務委員會; pinyin: Qiáo Wù Wěiyuánhuì) is a cabinet-level council of the Executive Yuan of the Republic of China (Taiwan). Founded in 1926 in Guangzhou, its primary mission is to manage cultural, educational, economic, and informational exchanges between Taiwan and overseas Taiwanese and Chinese-descended communities worldwide.

History and Name Changes

OCAC has undergone several name changes reflecting Taiwan’s evolving political landscape:

NamePeriodNotes
Overseas Chinese Affairs CommissionOctober 1926 – April 2006Original name
Overseas Compatriot Affairs CommissionApril 2006 – 1 September 2012Renamed under Chen Shui-bian to “avoid being confused as a governmental body of the PRC”
Overseas Chinese Affairs Commission1 September 2012 – 12 November 2012Brief interim
Overseas Community Affairs CouncilSince 12 November 2012Current name; reverted under KMT after 2012 election

In November 2012, a controversy arose when OCAC was found using simplified Chinese characters in some teaching materials. Facing budget-freezing threats from DPP legislators, the director relented and the agency was renamed “ROC (Taiwan) Overseas Community Affairs Council” to emphasize its Taiwan identity.

Mission and Scope

OCAC’s remit extends beyond Taiwanese expatriates to include all ethnic Taiwanese and Chinese living abroad who identify with the Republic of China (ROC). The council serves as the primary liaison between the Taiwan government and overseas communities across:

  • Cultural exchange — cultural delegations, heritage festivals, traditional arts
  • Education — Mandarin/Taiwanese/Hakka language programs, overseas Chinese schools, youth study tours
  • Economic and trade — business chambers, investment promotion, credit guarantee funds
  • Information services — certification, documentation, digital platforms
  • Community support — emergency assistance, annual conferences, youth ambassador programs

Overseas Presence

OCAC maintains overseas service points across six regions:

  • Asia — including Southeast Asia, South Asia, Middle East
  • Oceania — Australia, New Zealand, Pacific islands
  • Europe — EU countries, UK, Eastern Europe
  • North America — United States, Canada, Mexico
  • Central & South America — Latin American countries
  • Africa — Sub-Saharan African nations

OCAC offices may serve in addition to the Taipei Economic and Cultural Representative Offices (TECRO), providing complementary community-level services.

Services for Overseas Communities

Certification Services

  • Overseas Compatriot Identity Certificate (with nationality or ethnic Chinese proof)
  • Passport endorsements for overseas status
  • Military service exemption certifications

Education and Youth Programs

  • Taiwan Center for Mandarin Learning — language promotion
  • Formosa Association of Student Cultural Ambassadors (FASCA) — overseas youth cultural exchange
  • Overseas youth study tours and language programs
  • Subsidies for overseas Chinese schools to visit Taiwan
  • Digital online teaching resources

Business and Trade

  • Overseas Chinese chambers of commerce support
  • Investment in Taiwan facilitation
  • Overseas Credit Guarantee Fund

Community Support

  • Emergency Assistance Associations — crisis support for overseas compatriots
  • Continental Annual Conferences — regional community gatherings
  • Seminars of Overseas Community — visiting delegation programs
  • i-Taiwan Window — digital platform for overseas engagement

Relationship to Taiwanese-American Communities

OCAC plays a significant role in the Taiwanese-American experience in Southern California and beyond:

  • Funding and support for Taiwanese-American cultural organizations such as Taiwan Center Foundation of the Greater Los Angeles
  • Cultural delegation sponsorship — e.g., the Heritage Week Cultural Delegation hosted by Taiwan Center (2024, 2025)
  • Education program coordination — Taiwan School courses, teacher training, textbook distribution
  • Certification services — identity certificates, document authentication for Taiwanese-Americans
  • Community event facilitation — annual conferences, cultural festivals, youth programs

Notable Interactions

  • Ken Wu (吳兆峯) served as an advisor to OCAC for the Los Angeles area
  • Hsu Hsin-hung (許信宏) raised concerns at OCAC meetings about cross-strait terminology, advocating for “two countries” framing over “mainland” language
  • OCAC’s Los Angeles Service Center (http://ocac.gov.tw/OCAC/SubSites/Home.aspx?site=96775ef4-842d-4e5b-9491-abf5119661d1) provides localized services for Southern California’s Taiwanese-American community
