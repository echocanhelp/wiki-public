# Wang Gui-Rong / Kenjohn Wang (王桂榮)

**Wang Gui-Rong / Kenjohn Wang (王桂榮)** is identified in the official history of the [[taiwan-center-foundation-of-greater-los-angeles-大洛杉磯台灣會館基金會|Taiwan Center Foundation of Greater Los Angeles (大洛杉磯台灣會館基金會)]] as a key founding benefactor of the Taiwan Center in Southern California.

## Identity Snapshot

- Chinese name: 王桂榮
- Romanized name: Wang Gui-Rong
- English name used in Taiwan Center materials: Kenjohn Wang / Guirong Wang
- Community role documented by Taiwan Center: founding property donor and namesake of the Taiwan Center library
- Institutional context: Taiwan Center Foundation of Greater Los Angeles / 大洛杉磯台灣會館基金會

## Role in the Taiwan Center Founding

The Taiwan Center's official introduction states that, in 1998, Overseas Community Affairs Council commissioner 王桂榮 donated property in Rosemead, California. This donation formed part of the institutional foundation for the Taiwan Center project in Southern California.

The same official history states that more than forty Taiwanese organizations and about one thousand community members joined a US$1 million fundraising effort, with 吳澧培 (Wu Li-Pei) serving as fundraising convener. On June 12, 1998, the 南加州台灣會館基金會 was established as a nonprofit institution to own and manage the Southern California Taiwan Center. The institution later changed its name to 大洛杉磯台灣會館 in 2001.

## Library Legacy

The official Taiwan Center facility page identifies the **王桂榮圖書館** as a major cultural facility within the center. The library is described as holding more than 10,000 Taiwan-related books across art, history, literature, music, language, humanities, and related subjects.

The facility page also lists multiple publications connected to 王桂榮, including Chinese-language memoir titles and an English title, *Kenjohn Wang's Personal Memoir: Taiwan's Own Nobel Prize*, with the author listed as Guirong Wang.

## Historical Significance

Within the Taiwan Center's own institutional narrative, 王桂榮's significance lies in converting individual community philanthropy into a durable Taiwanese American public institution. His property donation helped make possible a physical center that became a cultural, educational, archival, and organizational hub for Taiwanese American life in Greater Los Angeles.
