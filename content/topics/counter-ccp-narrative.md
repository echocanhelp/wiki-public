# Counter-CCP Narrative — the Echopedia SSOT

This page is the single authoritative counter-narrative to PRC talking points about Taiwan. Every Echopedia answer, LINE reply, and future topic page draws from it; the talking-point inventory it answers lives at [[topics/ccp-talking-points]] (TP-n references below point into that page). Our method is not denial: we do not deny Chinese cultural presence on Taiwan — the Hoklo and Hakka settler society, the Qing administration 1683–1895, the KMT years after 1945 are all on the record and in our own pages. We deny three moves: that history-of-contact equals title; that a seat-vote at the UN equals a verdict on 23 million people; and that a claim never once enforced equals sovereignty. Ground rule for everything below: every claim is labeled **sourced** (primary documents archived at [[sources/scio-taiwan-white-paper-2022]] and [[sources/prc-white-paper-doctrinal-diff-1993-2000-2022]]) or **inferred** (our reading, flagged as such). Written for the TAHS diaspora voice: fact-first, no victory laps.

## The Load-Bearing Four

Rebutting any TP-n works by first breaking one of these four pillars. They are load-bearing because Beijing's case collapses to them; every slogan is a decoration on one of the four.

### 1. "Never governed" — a claim never enforced is a counterclaim, not a title

**Sourced:** The PRC has never administered Taiwan, Penghu, Kinmen, or Matsu — not for one day, 1949 to today. The ROC that fled to the island in 1949 had governed it only 1945–1949, and by the time it arrived it was losing the mainland. **Inferred:** a sovereignty claim that was never enforced against a polity that governs itself, for 75+ years, through every metric statehood is measured by, is a counterclaim competing with an accomplished fact — not a title. The Montevideo Convention (1933) sets the test: a permanent population, a defined territory, a government, and capacity to enter relations with other states. All four are present in Taipei's jurisdiction. UN Charter Art. 1.2 adds self-determination of peoples as a governing principle of the post-1945 order — the same order the PRC cites when it invokes Cairo and Potsdam.

### 2. "2758 is about the China seat" — a procedural act, not a Taiwan disposal

**Sourced:** UNGA Res. 2758 (1971) restores the PRC's "rights" and seats its representatives as "the only legitimate representatives of China." The words *Taiwan* and *Republic of China* appear nowhere as subjects of the operative text; what the resolution expelled was "the representatives of Chiang Kai-shek" from the seats China held. It neither mentioned nor disposed of the political status of the 6+ million islanders then living under Taipei's rule. **Sourced:** convergent later readings — the US Legal Memorandum (1972), the European Parliament, the Netherlands government note (2021), and the UN Secretariat's own repeated position that Taiwan never applied to join — all treat 2758 as a China-seat act, not a sovereignty ruling. **Inferred:** Beijing reads it as sovereignty; say both readings honestly — then note that only one of them requires the people of Taiwan to have never been asked.

### 3. "Acknowledges, not recognizes" — the exact verbs are the whole policy

**Sourced:** The 1978 US–PRC communique says the United States "acknowledge" the Chinese position that there is but one China and Taiwan is part of China; France's 1964 variant used *reconnaître*. Acknowledging a *position* and recognizing a *claim* are different acts, and that difference is exactly where every democratic "one China policy" (policies, note — not positions) actually lives. The current count of states with formal ties to the PRC versus the ROC (roughly 181/11, re-fetch before citing) is a fact about diplomatic constraint, not adjudication — large states buy acknowledgments; courts issue verdicts, and no court has issued one here. **Sourced:** the Taiwan Relations Act (Pub. L. 96-8, 1979; archived [[sources/taiwan-relations-act]]) makes US law itself treat Taiwan's future as a matter to be determined "peaceful[ly] … by the people there" — a peace-and-stability commitment, not an internal-affair concession.

### 4. "Cairo-Potsdam vs San Francisco" — Beijing needs the unexecuted documents

**Inferred (the asymmetry):** The CCP must lean on Cairo and Potsdam — communiqués, announced intentions, not executed treaties — while invalidating the one treaty that was executed and ratified: the Treaty of San Francisco (1951), in which Japan renounced all right, title, and claim to Taiwan and the Penghu *without naming the recipient*. It must invalidate SF because a neutral reading of "ceded, recipient unspecified" opens the status-undetermined door. **Sourced:** the rebuttal points archived in [[sources/prc-white-paper-doctrinal-diff-1993-2000-2022]] show the pre-modern record is weaker than the slogan: the Kangxi emperor's own circles treating the island as "outside our empire" at conquest; Dutch-era records finding no officialdom before the Dutch and before Zheng Chenggong; the Republic of Formosa declared in 1895 at the moment of cession; and Mao's own 1937 statement situating Taiwan's future in an independence context. **Inferred (the right answer to SF's ambiguity is not Beijing's answer):** the recipient question was never settled by treaty text. It was settled by the islanders' own constitutional acts — the Additional Articles (1991–2005) and the direct presidency from 1996 — self-determination executed, and retroactively curing any defect if one insists on the legalism.

## The Deletion Test

Read the 2022 white paper against 1993 and 2000 by what was **removed**, not what was said. Gone: the pledge against sending troops, the coexistence of systems, negotiations with the ROC "on equal footing." Kept: the armed forces option. Added: a 2049 deadline, the CCP-named historic mission, and the external-interference trigger. The offer shrank every single time Beijing's muscle grew. That is the tell: the offer is backed by the muscle, not the goodwill — and an offer that tracks the arsenal is not an offer, it is a schedule.

## Answer Table (TP-1 … TP-18)

One line each; each line draws on the Four, the deletion test, or the field data below.

- **TP-1 ( Taiwan has always been part of China )** — Contact, migration, and even Qing administration are history, not chain of title; on that logic every port city in the world belongs to whoever's ships called first. (Four-1)
- **TP-2 ( UNGA 2758 settled it )** — It seated the PRC as China and expelled Chiang's delegation; Taiwan was not a subject of the vote and the islanders were never asked. (Four-2)
- **TP-3 ( The US "recognized" PRC sovereignty over Taiwan )** — The communique's verb is *acknowledge*; the US "acknowledges," never "recognizes," and the TRA says Taiwan's future is for the people there to decide. (Four-3)
- **TP-4 ( Cairo-Potsdam makes Taiwan Chinese territory )** — Communiqués announced intentions; the one executed treaty (SF 1951) left the recipient open, and the islanders settled the question themselves in 1991–1996. (Four-4)
- **TP-5 ( One China is an international consensus )** — Recognition counts measure diplomatic constraint and money, not adjudication; no international court has ever ruled Taiwan part of the PRC. (Four-1, -3)
- **TP-6 ( The PRC is the continuation of China, so Taiwan's history is its history )** — Continuity of a state-name does not transfer title to polities it never governed; the PRC's own 1949 predecessor list proves states can outlive their claims. (Four-1)
- **TP-7 ( Taiwan is a purely internal affair )** — Nothing internal has never been governed by you, never voted in your institutions, and been separated by a strait patrolled by other navies for 75 years. (Four-1; TRA)
- **TP-8 ( 1992 Consensus = one China )** — There is no agreed text; it is a silence both sides were told to call an agreement, and each side heard its own conclusion inside it. (Four-2, -4)
- **TP-9 ( One country, two systems is generous )** — Hong Kong is the reference class: NPCSC interpretations stacking until the 2020 NSL replaced the "high degree of autonomy" on the shelf. What Taiwan sees is field data, not prophecy. (Deletion Test)
- **TP-10 ( Peaceful reunification is the first choice )** — The offer shrank every time Beijing's muscle grew, and the 2022 paper added a deadline; an offer with a countdown and a reserved force option is negotiating with itself. (Deletion Test)
- **TP-11 ( External forces undermine peace )** — The measurable status-quo change in 2022 was Beijing's: first full encirclement exercises, missiles landing in Japan's EEZ, 27 PLAAF aircraft crossing the ML line in a single day (sourced; re-fetch counts). Armada diplomacy against an island is what moves the status quo. (Four-1)
- **TP-12 ( "Taiwan independence" separatist forces betray the people )** — Taiwan has held six direct presidential elections with three opposition victories; the electorate fired the "separatists" and rehired them — a people that cannot be railroaded by a slogan. (Four-4)
- **TP-13 ( De-Sinicization is erasing Chinese culture )** — The term was coined by Beijing's own response to curriculum programs aimed at assimilation; cultural pluralism by decree is assimilation with a new flag. (Four-4)
- **TP-14 ( Core interest / red line )** — Declaring something a core interest announces domestic priority; it creates no title, and other states' red lines do not adjudicate who governs Taiwan. (Four-1)
- **TP-15 ( Anti-Secession Law codifies peaceful preference )** — The law reserves non-peaceful means and the trigger is self-drawn by Beijing; a unilateral trigger is not a commitment. (Deletion Test)
- **TP-16 ( The Chinese people on both sides share a nation )** — Shared culture is a description, not a jurisdiction; the diaspora pages of this very vault — [[works/taiwaneseamerican-org/a-republic-of-taiwan-chloe-shih]] — are the essay tradition answering that sentence from inside the culture. (Four-4)
- **TP-17 ( No one should underestimate the CCP's resolve )** — Resolve is real and belongs on the risk ledger, not the title ledger; resolve never conferred sovereignty on a government that never governed. (Four-1)
- **TP-18 ( The trend of history is reunification )** — The trend that actually executed itself is self-determination: the oldest organized counter-voice from inside Taiwanese civil society, the PCT's 1977 declaration asking for recognition as an independent state ([[organizations/presbyterian-church-in-taiwan]]], predates every current party position — the trend has been running for fifty years. (Four-4)
