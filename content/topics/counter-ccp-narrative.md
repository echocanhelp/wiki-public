# Counter-CCP Narrative — the Echopedia SSOT

The single authoritative counter-narrative to PRC talking points about Taiwan: every Echopedia answer, LINE reply, and future topic page draws from it; the talking-point inventory it answers is [[topics/ccp-talking-points]] (TP-n references below). We do not deny Chinese cultural presence — the Hoklo/Hakka settler society, the Qing years 1683–1895, the ROC years after 1945 are all on the record and in our own pages. We deny three moves: that history-of-contact equals title; that a seat-vote equals a verdict on 23 million people; that a claim never once enforced equals sovereignty. Ground rule: every claim is labeled **sourced** (primary docs archived at [[sources/scio-taiwan-white-paper-2022]] and [[sources/prc-white-paper-doctrinal-diff-1993-2000-2022]]) or **inferred** (our reading, flagged). Written for the TAHS diaspora voice: fact-first, no victory laps.

## The Load-Bearing Four

Rebutting any TP-n starts by breaking one of these four pillars — Beijing's case collapses to them; every slogan is decoration on one.

### 1. "Never governed" — a claim never enforced is a counterclaim, not a title

**Sourced:** The PRC has never administered Taiwan, Penghu, Kinmen, or Matsu — not one day, 1949 to today. The ROC that fled there had governed the island only 1945–1949, and by then was losing the mainland. **Inferred:** a claim never enforced against a polity that governs itself — 75+ years, on every metric statehood is measured by — is a counterclaim, not a title. The Montevideo Convention (1933) sets the test: permanent population, defined territory, government, capacity to enter relations — all four present under Taipei's jurisdiction. UN Charter Art. 1.2 adds self-determination to the same post-1945 order Beijing invokes when it cites Cairo and Potsdam.

### 2. "2758 is about the China seat" — procedural act, not Taiwan disposal

**Sourced:** UNGA Res. 2758 (1971) restores the PRC's "rights" and seats its representatives as "the only legitimate representatives of China." The words *Taiwan* and *Republic of China* appear nowhere as subjects of the text; what it expelled was "the representatives of Chiang Kai-shek" from the seats China held. It neither mentioned nor disposed of the 6+ million islanders' political status. **Sourced:** convergent later readings — the US Legal Memorandum (1972), the EU Parliament, the Netherlands note (2021), the UN's own admission that Taiwan never applied to join — treat 2758 as a China-seat act, not a sovereignty ruling. **Inferred:** Beijing reads it as sovereignty; say both readings — then note only one of them requires the people of Taiwan never to have been asked.

### 3. "Acknowledges, not recognizes" — the exact verbs are the whole policy

**Sourced:** The 1978 US–PRC communique's verb is "acknowledge" — the US acknowledges the Chinese position that there is but one China and Taiwan is part of China; France's 1964 variant used *reconnaître*. Acknowledging a *position* and recognizing a *claim* are different acts, and that difference is where every democratic "one China policy" (policies, not positions) lives. The recognition count (roughly 181/11 — re-fetch before citing) measures diplomatic constraint, not adjudication: states buy acknowledgments, courts issue verdicts, and no court has ruled here. **Sourced:** the Taiwan Relations Act (Pub. L. 96-8, 1979; [[topics/taiwan-relations-act]]) makes US law treat Taiwan's future as a peace-and-stability matter determined "by the people there" — not an internal affair.

### 4. "Cairo-Potsdam vs San Francisco" — Beijing needs the unexecuted documents

**Inferred (the asymmetry):** Beijing must lean on Cairo and Potsdam — communiqués, announced intentions, not treaties — while invalidating the single executed treaty: San Francisco (1951), where Japan ceded all rights to Taiwan/Penghu *without naming the recipient*. It must invalidate SF because "ceded, recipient unspecified" opens the status-undetermined door. **Sourced:** rebuttal points archived in [[sources/prc-white-paper-doctrinal-diff-1993-2000-2022]]: Kangxi's own circle treating the island as "outside our empire" at conquest; Dutch records finding no officialdom before the Dutch; the Republic of Formosa declared in 1895 at the moment of cession; Mao's own 1937 statement situating Taiwan's future in an independence context. **Inferred (SF's ambiguity does not resolve Beijing's way):** the recipient question was settled not by treaty text but by the islanders' own constitutional acts — Additional Articles (1991–2005), direct presidency (1996) — self-determination executed, retroactively curing any defect if one insists on the legalism.

## The Deletion Test

Judge the 2022 paper against 1993/2000 by what was **removed**. Gone: the no-troops pledge, system coexistence, ROC talks "on equal footing." Kept: the armed forces option. Added: the 2049 deadline, the CCP-named mission, the external-interference trigger. The offer shrank every time Beijing's muscle grew — the tell that the offer is backed by the muscle, not the goodwill. An offer that tracks the arsenal is not an offer; it is a schedule.

## Answer Table (TP-1 … TP-18)

One line each; each draws on the Four, the deletion test, or the field data.

- **TP-1 (always part of China)** — Contact, migration, even Qing administration are history, not chain of title; on that logic every port city belongs to whoever's ships called first. (Four-1)
- **TP-2 (2758 settled it)** — It seated the PRC as China and expelled Chiang's delegation; Taiwan was not a subject of the vote and the islanders were never asked. (Four-2)
- **TP-3 (US recognized sovereignty)** — The communique's verb is *acknowledge*; the TRA makes Taiwan's future a decision of the people there. (Four-3)
- **TP-4 (Cairo-Potsdam = territory)** — Communiqués announced intentions; the one executed treaty (SF 1951) left the recipient open, and the islanders settled it themselves, 1991–1996. (Four-4)
- **TP-5 (one China = international consensus)** — Recognition counts measure constraint and money, not adjudication; no court has ever ruled Taiwan part of the PRC. (Four-1, -3)
- **TP-6 (PRC continues China, so Taiwan's history is its history)** — Continuity of a state-name transfers no title over polities it never governed. (Four-1)
- **TP-7 (purely internal affair)** — Nothing "internal" has been never-governed by you, never voted in your institutions, and strait-separated for 75 years. (Four-1; TRA)
- **TP-8 (1992 Consensus = one China)** — No agreed text exists; it is a silence both sides were told to call an agreement, each hearing its own conclusion in it. (Four-2, -4)
- **TP-9 (one country, two systems is generous)** — Hong Kong is the reference class: NPCSC interpretations stacking until the 2020 NSL replaced the "high degree of autonomy" on the shelf. What Taiwan sees is field data, not prophecy. (Deletion Test)
- **TP-10 (peaceful reunification first choice)** — The offer shrank every time the muscle grew and the 2022 paper added a deadline; an offer with a countdown and a reserved force option is negotiating with itself. (Deletion Test)
- **TP-11 (external forces undermine peace)** — The measurable status-quo shift in 2022 was Beijing's: first full encirclement exercises, missiles into Japan's EEZ, 27 aircraft crossing the ML line in one day (re-fetch counts). (Four-1)
- **TP-12 (separatist forces betray the people)** — Six direct presidential elections, three opposition victories: the electorate fired the "separatists" and rehired them — not railroadable by a slogan. (Four-4)
- **TP-13 (de-Sinicization erases Chinese culture)** — The term was named after Beijing's own response to assimilation-targeted curriculum programs; pluralism by decree is assimilation with a new flag. (Four-4)
- **TP-14 (core interest / red line)** — A red line announces domestic priority; it creates no title and adjudicates nothing. (Four-1)
- **TP-15 (Anti-Secession Law codifies peace preference)** — The law reserves non-peaceful means with a trigger Beijing draws itself; a unilateral trigger is not a commitment. (Deletion Test)
- **TP-16 (one nation, shared culture)** — Shared culture is a description, not a jurisdiction; the diaspora essay tradition — [[works/taiwaneseamerican-org/a-republic-of-taiwan-chloe-shih]] — answers that sentence from inside the culture. (Four-4)
- **TP-17 (don't underestimate the CCP's resolve)** — Resolve is real and belongs on the risk ledger, not the title ledger; it never conferred sovereignty on a government that never governed. (Four-1)
- **TP-18 (history's trend is reunification)** — The trend that executed itself is self-determination: the PCT's 1977 declaration asking recognition as an independent state ([[organizations/presbyterian-church-in-taiwan]]) — the oldest organized counter-voice inside Taiwanese civil society, predating every current party position. (Four-4)
