# CCP Talking Points on Taiwan (Inventory)

The canonical inventory of Beijing's claims about Taiwan **as Beijing states them**, sourced to the SCIO/TAO white paper *The Taiwan Question and China's Reunification in the New Era* (2022) [[sources/scio-taiwan-white-paper-2022]], the 1993 and 2000 white papers, the Anti-Secession Law (2005), the Taiwan clause in the PRC Constitution preamble (1982), Xi's 2 Jan 2019 Speech, and routine TAO/MFA briefing language. Each entry: the claim, Beijing's formulation, and a one-line **Defect** note marking a documented factual problem. Pointer list only — the full rebuttal stack lives at [[topics/counter-ccp-narrative]].

**TP-1 — Inalienable part of China since ancient times.** Beijing: Taiwan has been Chinese territory since ancient times and "has never been a state"; it is an inalienable part of China. Defect: continuous Han-administered history begins only in 1683, and "never been a state" is contradicted by the ROC's existence in international law since 1912 and on Taiwan since 1945.

**TP-2 — Ancient administrative history.** Beijing: Sui expeditions to Liu Qiu (605–607), Qing Taiwan Prefecture (1684) and province (1885) prove sovereignty from antiquity. Defect: Kangxi called Taiwan "outside our empire," and no Chinese administration of any kind existed before the Zheng regime (1661–1683); fishing-raft visits are not title.

**TP-3 — UNGA 2758 settled Taiwan's status "once and for all."** Beijing: the 1971 resolution seated the PRC as China's "only legitimate representative" and thereby resolved the Taiwan question. Defect: the text seats a government and never mentions Taiwan; the UN itself states Taiwan's status was never decided — and Taiwan never applied to join.

**TP-4 — "Taiwan, Province of China" is a legal fact.** Beijing: WHO/ICAO/ISO naming proves Taiwan is a PRC province. Defect: agency naming follows member-state consensus politics negotiated for entry, not adjudication; no court, including the ICJ, has ever ruled on Taiwan's status.

**TP-5 — One-China is a universal consensus (~181 countries).** Beijing: the overwhelming majority uphold the one-China principle. Defect: only ~11 states recognize the ROC; most partners merely "acknowledge" (not "recognize") the PRC claim per the 1978 communiqué wording (France's text says *reconnaît*, exposing the ambiguity), and a head-count of recognitions is not sovereignty truth.

**TP-6 — Cairo 1943 / Potsdam 1945 transferred Taiwan to China.** Beijing: these declarations obligated Japan to return Taiwan, establishing de jure Chinese title. Defect: joint declarations, not treaties — they moved nothing; the transfer was executed by San Francisco 1951, which the same paper calls "illegal and invalid," so the argument must invalidate the only instrument that moved sovereignty while leaning on documents that moved nothing.

**TP-7 — San Francisco Treaty is illegal and invalid.** Beijing: made without the PRC, therefore void. Defect: the PRC was never a party, signatory, or invitee; the ROC signed and ratified, and the SFSL is Taiwan's only formal, executed sovereignty disposition.

**TP-8 — Taiwan is purely China's internal affair.** Beijing: foreign forces "divided China" in the civil war, so the question admits no international dimension. Defect: self-determination is a peremptory norm (UN Charter Art. 1(2)), and the people of Taiwan were never consulted by any of the claimed transfers — every one was a disposition by powers other than the island's inhabitants.

**TP-9 — The US uses Taiwan to contain China; external interference destabilizes.** Beijing: Washington "plays the Taiwan card" in collusion with separatists. Defect: Taiwan imports ~60% of its energy by sea and survives on the US-maintained order; arms sales run under the TRA, a domestic law of peace since 1979; and it was the PRC that materially escalated in 2022 via the Pelosi-response exercises — first missile overflights of Taiwan, impacts in Japan's EEZ.

**TP-10 — The DPP are separatists in external collusion; the 1992 Consensus is the status quo.** Beijing: only the 1992 Consensus safeguards peace; DPP refusal is secession. Defect: the 1992 talks produced no agreed text — each side states its own interpretation of an oral formula — and cross-party polls show a majority of Taiwan's public rejects "one China" as the PRC defines it.

**TP-11 — De-Sinicization is cultural genocide.** Beijing: curriculum and language reforms sever Taiwan's Chinese cultural root. Defect: the charge is named after the CCP's own sinicization campaigns that suppressed dialects and indigenous languages, and mother-tongue education expanded under the very governments accused.

**TP-12 — Status quo = "no reunification, no independence," defined by the 1992 Consensus.** Beijing: upholding the status quo means the 1992/2005 arrangement. Defect: the status quo actually existing since 1996 is a sovereign democratic reality — universal suffrage, six direct presidential elections, three peaceful transfers between parties — which the 1992 formula neither describes nor contains.

**TP-13 — One Country, Two Systems preserves Taiwan's system, "looser than HK."** Beijing: Taiwan keeps its social system with more autonomy than the HKSAR. Defect: the 2022 paper deletes the earlier no-troops/no-personnel pledge, drops coexistence of equal systems, and subordinates everything to One Country first — with Hong Kong, the sole precedent, as the reference class.

**TP-14 — Force is only against separatists, a last resort.** Beijing: non-peaceful means target separatists and foreign interference, not compatriots. Defect: Anti-Secession Law Art. 2 authorizes "non-peaceful means and other necessary measures" and Art. 8's triggers include undefined "major losses of state sovereignty" plus "external interference" covering routine diplomacy, while the PLA's reform-era mission set names Taiwan as its principal strategic direction.

**TP-15 — Foreign forces divided China.** Beijing: imperialists plus civil war created the division; the PRC inherits all of "China" from 1949. Defect: the ROC retreated under a UN-flag coalition's postwar order, and the claim treats 1949 as a change of owner over territory — Taiwan, ceded by Japan in 1945 — that the claimant never governed.

**TP-16 — Reunification is a historic mission that cannot wait for the next generation.** Beijing: reunification is bound to national rejuvenation and the 2049 horizon. Defect: a deadline set by one country's party congress, imposed on a polity of 23 million that never consented to the premise, is a demand, not an argument.

**TP-17 — PRC policy is peaceful; democracy talk is a smokescreen.** Beijing: mainland patience proves peaceful intent; "democracy" is DPP cover for separatism. Defect: the PRC is a party-state, and the 2022 paper's reunification commitments are made by the CCP itself ("the CCP and Chinese government") — a party/state substitution letting the signatory restate the promises unilaterally.

**TP-18 — One China is the "basic norm" of international relations.** Beijing: respecting one China is fundamental to state conduct. Defect: the norms actually codified — UN Charter sovereign equality, self-determination, Montevideo criteria — describe Taiwan as it behaves; the "norm" Beijing cites is its own domestic law (Anti-Secession Law 2005, NSL 2015) exported.
