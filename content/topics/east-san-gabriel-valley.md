# East San Gabriel Valley — the Little Taipei chapter

The east SGV — **Rowland Heights, Hacienda Heights, Diamond Bar, Walnut** (mostly *unincorporated*, i.e. no city government) — is where the Taiwanese immigration wave of the late 1980s built its own district after Monterey Park/Alhambra filled up. Because there was no municipal layer, **the community built its own civic infrastructure**: associations, festivals, schools foundations, dance federations, and a property manager that turned plazas into public squares. This hub stitches that chapter together.

## The settlement layer

- LA Times **1992**: Colima Rd. already "one of the most thoroughly [Asian commercial strips] in the West"; Rowland Heights ~30% Chinese, "about 80%" of them from Taiwan.
- LA Times **2007**: "Rowland Heights and Walnut are more than 50% Asian… **the dominant culture here is from Taiwan**" — and the noted absence: Taiwanese immigrants "never tried to re-create that night life here."
- Academic record (eScholarship dissertation): east-district community formed **late 1980s** in the four towns; "Taiwan American Association of East San Gabriel Valley" established **1998**; east-district identity consciously constructed in the early 1990s.

## The through-line

**The nightlife gap of 2007 became the night-market institution of 2026.** The first generation worked and raised kids; STC's countdown concerts filled the public-square hole; and in 2026 the founder — raised in Taiwan, "immigrated 43 years" — bought a dying 13-acre mall specifically to rebuild the Taiwan night market he remembered ("這個夢想終於實現了"). Between the two ends of that arc sit the same faces: a RHCA president negotiating school-dropoff parking with the County Sheriff, gala proceeds routed to FORUS teacher grants, and 300 dancers a year filling the San Gabriel Mission Playhouse for their 37th show.

**Identity note (recorded, not editorialized):** these institutions use 華人/Chinese vocabulary and the ROC 僑務 system (OCAC, 僑務委員, 經文處), yet their program content — 山地/aboriginal and Hakka dances, 《我們的田野》, Taiwan night-market food, Double Ten, "台灣的軟實力" — is steadily Taiwan-centered. The east-SGV establishment's identity is institutional-ROC in form and community-Taiwanese in content; both layers belong in the record.
