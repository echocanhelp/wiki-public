# Organization For The Support of Democratic Movement of Taiwan (台灣民主運動支援會)

## Identity Snapshot
- **English:** Organization For The Support of Democratic Movement of Taiwan
- **Chinese:** 台灣民主運動支援會
- **Era:** Contemporary
- **Geography:** Taiwanese American community
- **Core roles:** Community organization (TAH directory)

The Organization For The Support of Democratic Movement of Taiwan was founded in 1979 by a group of overseas Taiwanese youth — led by Lin Hsiao-hsin (known as ‘Old Lin’) — who carried on the spirit of the Defend Diaoyu Islands movement and redirected their energies toward supporting democratic and social reform in Taiwan. The organization was active across the U.S. Midwest and East Coast, hosting annual summer camps and symposiums to connect students and Chinese Americans who cared about Taiwan’s development. Members collectively analyzed cross-strait situations and strategized ways to support Taiwan’s democratic movements from abroad. The organization published a periodical called ‘Democratic Taiwan’ (民主台灣, also subtitled 台灣民主運動支援會通訊) as a key vehicle for communication and advocacy.

## Deepen pass (2026-09-14, vault-sourced)
The TAH story corpus holds the organization's own publications as community records:
- [[works/taiwaneseamericanhistory-org/newsletter-osdmt||民主台灣(通訊)]] — the support council's newsletter 民主台灣, cited 2016-03-23
- [[works/taiwaneseamericanhistory-org/publicationmagazines978||台灣民主運動支援會第12期通訊]] — newsletter issue 12, 01/1980
- [[works/taiwaneseamericanhistory-org/publications1145||葉島蕾事件專刊]] — special issue on the 葉島蕾 case, 05/1981

These place the organization's print advocacy in the 1979-1981 window immediately after its founding, alongside its Midwest/East Coast camps and symposiums.

Corroborating community memory: 吳木盛's essay [[works/taiwaneseamericanhistory-org/ourjourneys222|222. 秀才造反 / 吳木盛 /05/2016]] — a movement insider cataloguing 54 overseas Taiwanese magazines he collected across three decades — lists 民主台灣 among the papers circulating in the **late 1970s and early 1980s** (alongside 台灣青年、台獨、望春風、台灣通訊、人權通訊). This independently dates the support council's periodical to the same 1979-1984 window as the vault's own issue copies, and shows it was read across the diaspora's magazine ecosystem rather than in isolation.
