# Ann Arbor Taiwanese Association (安雅堡)

## Identity Snapshot
- **English:** Ann Arbor Taiwanese Association
- **Chinese:** 安雅堡
- **Era:** Contemporary
- **Geography:** Taiwanese American community
- **Core roles:** Community organization (TAH directory)

The Ann Arbor Taiwanese Association (AATA) is a community organization serving Taiwanese and Taiwanese American residents primarily in Ann Arbor, Michigan. It is a chapter of the national Taiwanese Association of America (TAA-USA) and falls within the TAA’s Mid-Western regional district. The chapter describes itself as a group that stands for the welfare of Taiwanese and Taiwanese Americans across diverse backgrounds.

## Role in the Community
- 1970 — Ann Arbor is named as one of the **28 founding chapters of the Taiwanese Association of America** in the TAA founding memoir: when the Formosan Club of America (later TAA) was established in New York on 1970-07-01, **Yu-chao Wu (吳玉超)** is recorded as Ann Arbor's founding chapter president, and the chapter's newsletter (with Detroit and East Lansing) is listed among the movement's early publications ([[works/taiwaneseamericanhistory-org/ourjourneys76-eng|Our Journeys 76 (EN) — The Birth and Growth of the Taiwanese Association of America]]).
- 1981-07-18 — the Ann Arbor 台灣同鄕會 (with the University of Michigan 台灣同學會) was central to the Chen Wen-chen memorial mobilization: after the Midwest 同鄉會 held Dr. Chen's memorial service in Ann Arbor, the CHEN WEN-CHEN MEMORIAL FUND was founded on the spot in the church basement, with Ann Arbor's 許永華 as a main nationwide donation聯絡人 (per memoir [[works/taiwaneseamericanhistory-org/ourjourneys321|Our Journeys 321 — 台風眼陳文成紀念專刊]] and [[works/taiwaneseamericanhistory-org/ourjourneys8|Our Journeys 8 by 林富文]]); the 1981-08-15 Midwest Summer Camp in Grand Rapids raised nearly $4,000 more in its name. Successor institution: [[organizations/professor-chen-wen-chen-memorial-foundation|Professor Chen Wen-chen Memorial Foundation]].
- The community's democratic-movement organizing in Ann Arbor is also memorialized in [[works/taiwaneseamericanhistory-org/ann-arbor-democratic|安雅堡台灣民主運動基金會]] and the chapter's own bulletin [[works/taiwaneseamericanhistory-org/news-ann-arbor|安雅堡台灣同鄉會快訊]].
- 1979 — two Michigan Taiwanese, I-hsiung Lin and Bernie Huang, wired funds to political prisoner Shih Ming-teh (施明德) at First Bank in Taipei **under the name of the Ann Arbor Taiwanese Association**; the transfer receipt is kept in the Chen Wen-chen database, and the Kuo Yu-hsin–published Taiwan Democracy Newsletter reported that the KMT interfered with Shih because of the sum (per memoir [[works/taiwaneseamericanhistory-org/ourjourneys321-eng|Our Journeys 321 (EN)]]). An early instance of the chapter's name being used for political solidarity action.
- 2016-11-20 — the adjacent Ann Arbor Taiwanese Church marked its 40th anniversary ([[works/taiwaneseamericanhistory-org/important2016-30|30. 40th Anniversary of Ann Arbor Taiwanese Church on 11/20/2016.]]), indicating a 1976 founding; church and 同鄉會 formed overlapping pillars of Ann Arbor community life (church record page: [[works/taiwaneseamericanhistory-org/ann-arbor-taiwanese-church|Ann Arbor Taiwanese Church]]).
