# Cornell Taiwanese Student Association

## Identity Snapshot
- **English:** Cornell Taiwanese Student Association
- **Chinese:** —
- **Era:** Contemporary
- **Geography:** Taiwanese American community
- **Core roles:** Community organization (TAH directory)

The Cornell Taiwanese Student Association (CTSA; 康乃爾臺灣同學會) is a registered student organization at Cornell University in Ithaca, New York. It is a non-profit, non-religious, and non-political group primarily oriented toward students from Taiwan, helping them navigate life and academics at Cornell.

## Role in the Community
- The movement-era predecessor at Cornell is documented in community memoir [[works/taiwaneseamericanhistory-org/ourjourneys310-eng|310. 伊薩卡臺灣同鄉會的簡介 (Mei-Hsi Chen)]]. The Cornell Taiwanese student gathering began in the mid-1960s (Joseph H. Chen arrived for his Ph.D. in 1965; earlier figures included Dr. Chao-hsiung Yeh and Taiwanese Ph.D. students Shu-ho Wang and Yang-yang Wu), centered on Taiwanese cuisine, mutual aid, and welcoming new students.
- Per the same memoir: after Mainland China opened up and Chinese students swelled the "Chinese student's association," Taiwanese students consolidated as the "Cornell University Taiwanese Student's Association"; at its peak the group counted nearly 300 members including families. In the 60s–70s the association communicated in Taiwanese; later generations mixed Mandarin in.
- Cornell also appears in the Hakka movement record: [[works/taiwaneseamericanhistory-org/ourjourneys110-eng|110. 話說美洲台灣客家社團 (Hsing-fu Wang)]] lists 3rd-term THA-USA president Yung-pin Liu as based in Cornell, NY (Nov. 1991).
- The Ithaca Taiwanese hometown association continues the same community lineage on campus: [[works/taiwaneseamericanhistory-org/ithaca-taa|Ithaca, NY / TAA 綺色佳(伊薩卡)臺灣同鄉會 (2017)]].
- Cornell's place in the movement record extends beyond the association itself: community memoir [[works/taiwaneseamericanhistory-org/our-journeys-375|375. 1983年李登輝訪問紐約州首府 (08/2021)]] recalls how Lee Teng-hui's 1995 return to his alma mater Cornell — "民之所欲，長在我心" — 轟動全球, a defining moment for the Cornell Taiwanese community.
- Earlier Cornell Taiwanese figures surface in church memoir [[works/taiwaneseamericanhistory-org/our-journeys-350|350. 美國舊金山灣區台灣基督教會史料簡介 (何義麟, 11/2019)]]: 黃介山 of 樹林, a 長老教會 believer, first studied at 康乃爾大學 before transferring to Stanford, later anchoring the Bay Area Taiwanese church community.
- Cornell was also a performance stop for the movement's cultural network: [[works/taiwaneseamericanhistory-org/15-e7-be-85-e5-be-b9-e6-96-af-e7-89-b9-e8-87-ba-e7-81-a3-e5-90-8c-e9-84-95-e6-9c|15. Taiwanese Choral Society of Rochester 羅徹斯特臺灣同鄕會愛鄕合唱團 (2014-12)]] records the 愛鄕合唱團 being invited to sing at Cornell University.
