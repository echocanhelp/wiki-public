# Taiwanese Student Association at NC State

## Identity Snapshot
- **English:** Taiwanese Student Association at NC State
- **Chinese:** —
- **Era:** Contemporary
- **Geography:** Taiwanese American community
- **Core roles:** Community organization (TAH directory)

The Taiwanese Student Association at NC State (NCSU TSA) is a student organization at North Carolina State University in Raleigh, North Carolina, serving the Taiwanese student community on campus. The organization maintains an active Instagram presence under the handle @tsaatncsu, where it has accumulated over 400 followers and more than 140 posts, and a Facebook group under the name “NC State TSA.” No dedicated website, Linktree, or publicly accessible GetInvolved directory listing with a mission statement could be confirmed through available first-party sources. NC State’s Office of Global Engagement has noted a consistent Taiwanese student population on campus, with students from Taiwan representing one of the university’s larger international student groups as of the mid-2010s, providing context for the organization’s community base.

## Role in the Community

A community memoir of the North Carolina Taiwanese community ([[works/taiwaneseamericanhistory-org/our-journeys-381|北卡台灣同鄉會回憶（Our Journeys 381）]]) supplies the historical bedrock beneath today's association:

- NC State (NCSU), focused on engineering, agriculture, and technology, drew a large Taiwanese student presence — the memoir recalls Taiwanese students there once numbering over a hundred, in a Research Triangle area (Raleigh–Chapel Hill–Durham) that became a high-tech employment base for the community.
- The memoir records an earlier organized Taiwanese student body at NCSU: Dr. Kuo-Ching Lin 林國慶, nicknamed the "國父" (founding father) of the NCSU 台灣學生會 for his power of mobilization and critique, co-founded an activity library and regular reading groups that drew many graduate students into the independence discussion. He and Dr. Pei-Hung Kuo 郭倍宏 were principals in the campus poster incident (海報事件); the memoir names fellow alumnus 劉格正 as one of the people the FBI interviewed over KMT spying on student organizations — [[works/taiwaneseamericanhistory-org/our-journeys-381|Our Journeys 381]].
- Lung-Jung Ko 高龍榮, then a doctoral student at NCSU, served as the first president (社長) of 《台灣學生》, a magazine that circulated over 4,000 copies, while Ying-Yuan Li 李應元 (UNC) was its first publisher.
- The broader NC Taiwanese community anchored around the 北卡台灣同鄉會 (founded 1971, publisher of the monthly 台訊 for 100+ issues), which hosted speakers such as Chen Lung-chu, Shih Ming, Irene Hsiu (艾琳達), Hung Cheh-sheng, John S.M. Chen, June Cheng-te Hsu, Tangloh Chen and Thomas Su — the movement milieu campus TSA groups historically fed into — [[works/taiwaneseamericanhistory-org/activities-of-taa-nc|TAA/NC 北卡台灣同鄉會的活動]]、[[works/taiwaneseamericanhistory-org/enewsletter-taa-north-carolina-chapter|TAA/NC 通訊]]. Community reminiscence also records 吳焜裕 (UNC, later DPP 不分區立委, food-safety advocate) and, in the 80s, 李宗敬's home phone line replaying Radio Taiwan News for 鄉親 (call-in 台灣之音) — [[works/taiwaneseamericanhistory-org/our-journeys-381|Our Journeys 381]].
