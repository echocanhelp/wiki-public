# Center for Taiwan International Relations, CTIR (台灣國際關係中心)

## Identity Snapshot
- **English:** Center for Taiwan International Relations, CTIR
- **Chinese:** 台灣國際關係中心
- **Era:** Contemporary
- **Geography:** Taiwanese American community (Washington, D.C.)
- **Core roles:** Community organization (TAH directory)

The Center for Taiwan International Relations (CTIR) was formally established on December 15, 1988, in Washington, D.C. Its core purposes include researching and promoting Taiwan's independent sovereignty and equal standing in the international community, as well as developing diplomatic relations with both developing and advanced nations. CTIR also aims to build a think tank to cultivate diplomatic talent among Taiwanese at home and abroad, and to facilitate exchanges between Taiwan's social movement organizations and counterpart groups in other countries.

## Role in the Community
Recorded in the TAH *Our Journey* memoir corpus ([[works/taiwaneseamericanhistory-org/ourjourneys47|Our Journey #47]], [[works/taiwaneseamericanhistory-org/ourjourneys254|Our Journey #254]]; org record [[works/taiwaneseamericanhistory-org/center-for-taiwan-international-relations-ctir|29. CTIR 台灣國際關係中心]]):

- **Leadership:** Directed by 蔡武雄 (Tsai Wu-xiong), who worked at the U.S. Library of Congress.
- **Passport birthplace campaign:** CTIR, together with FAPA 台灣人公共事務會, FAHR 全美台灣人權協會, TAA 全美台灣同鄉會, and WUFI 台灣獨立建國聯盟, treated the effort to have U.S. passports of Taiwanese Americans state "Taiwan" instead of "China" as a key joint diplomatic task at organization caucus meetings. This built on an early lobbying effort by diplomatic elder 陳翠玉, who had campaigned to have "Formosa" rather than "China" entered as her birthplace.
- **White House confrontation (early 1992):** In a White House briefing for Taiwanese Americans, director 蔡武雄 produced the U.S. passport of a Ukrainian colleague at the Library of Congress — showing birthplace "Ukraine," not "Soviet Union" — and pressed 包道格 (Dennis Hellmer), then Asia affairs director of the NSC, to apply the same treatment to Taiwanese. CTIR members thereafter lobbied the White House, State Department, AIT, and the House and Senate foreign affairs committees on the issue.
- **1994 legislative outcome:** The campaign reached a milestone when the Senate-passed Roth (佩爾) amendment to the 1994 State Department Authorization Act — allowing birthplace "Taiwan" on U.S. passports — passed the joint House–Senate foreign affairs committee debate without challenge (reported April 19, via a CTIR Washington dispatch).
- **1988–89 NATPA partnership:** CTIR co-hosted with NATPA the first Taiwanese-identity-based reception in Washington for Taiwan-studies scholars attending the 41st Association for Asian Studies annual meeting; NATPA's memoir names 蔡武雄 as CTIR's person in charge.
