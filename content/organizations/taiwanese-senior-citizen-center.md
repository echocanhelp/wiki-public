# Taiwanese Senior Citizen Center (紐約台灣老人聯合會)

## Identity Snapshot
- **English:** Taiwanese Senior Citizen Center
- **Chinese:** 紐約台灣老人聯合會
- **Era:** Contemporary
- **Geography:** Taiwanese American community
- **Core roles:** Community organization (TAH directory)

The Taiwanese Senior Citizen Center, also known as the New York Taiwan Senior Center, serves the Taiwanese senior community in the greater New York area, particularly the Flushing, Queens region. It was formed through the merger of three longstanding Taiwanese senior organizations — the Taiwanese Christian Elders Association, the Changchun Association, and the Songbai Association — to consolidate resources and enable members to access U.S. government benefits for retirees, including daily nutritious lunches, medical care, and activity center programs. The center currently serves approximately 500 members and offers physical fitness activities, arts and leisure classes, social welfare referrals, and health seminars. Members have also formed a choir, a Taiwanese opera (歌仔戲) troupe, and dance groups that perform regularly in the community.

## Role in the Community
Absorbed from the founding memoir by 林炎誠 (Oct 2017), preserved in the corpus at [[works/taiwaneseamericanhistory-org/ourjourneys316|316. 紐約台灣會館老人中心 / 林炎誠 /10/2017]]; bibliographic record also at [[works/taiwaneseamericanhistory-org/taiwanese-senior-citizen-center|15. Taiwanese Senior Citizen Center 紐約台灣老人聯合會]] (2015).

- **Predecessors (1988–1993):** three senior groups formed in New York by first-generation Taiwanese elders — 大紐約區台灣人基督徒長輩會 (長輩會, Fridays at the Taiwanese Club), 大紐約區長春會 (長春會), and 紐約台灣松柏會 (松柏會).
- **2001-12-17:** under Club chairman 李汝城, the Taiwan Center Senior Citizens Club (台灣會館鄉親俱樂部) was founded at the Taiwanese Club; 李鵬 was the first secretary-general, and 李汝城, 尤欽德, and 蔡明峯 ([[people/martin-tsai|蔡明峰]]) registered it with New York State. It later became the Taiwanese American Senior Center (TASC).
- **2003-01-14:** at the Chinese Cultural & Community Center, 張俊雄 (長輩會), 何錦華 (長春會), and 陳英隆 (松柏會) agreed to dissolve their three associations and merge into 大紐約區台灣老人聯合會 (Taiwanese Senior Citizen Center). On 2003-03-12 [[people/dr-hong-tien-lai|賴弘典]] took office as the first merged-chairman and opened the Club to all elders under the TASC umbrella; the joint declaration of 2003-03-21 was signed by 張俊雄, 何錦華, 陳英隆, and 周秀琴. 林炎誠 compiled the initial member roll (~300).
- **Late 2003:** a management committee was seated with 賴弘典 as chair and 林秀合 as executive director; seven commissioners drawn from Club directors (陳春蘭, 蔡明峰, 尤欽德) and the former associations (何錦華, 吳淑慧, 周秀琴, 吳發鎮). Operations began January 2004. The 長輩會 later withdrew to run Friday worship independently; the center moved to a chairman system, four activity days a week.
- **Chairmen in sequence:** 何錦華, 吳發鎮, 彭吉浦, 周秀琴, 陳金英, 黃才春, 胡慶祥, 林淑滿. A board of directors (annual $500奉献; chairman $5,000) was created for income, with 陳隆豊 serving long-term as board chairman. Membership requires age 60+ ($60/year, later $100).
- **Community roles:** members are broadly pro-Taiwan retired Taiwanese Americans; they demonstrated at the UN, danced at the International Immigration Day parade on Sixth Avenue (2005-07-16), joined the Lunar New Year parade in Flushing, and perform at nursing homes and other associations' events.

HOLD: merger framing — TAH directory blurb says the Center itself "was formed through the merger" and serves ~500, while the 2017 memoir dates the merger decision to 2003 and the initial roll to ~300; both kept (directory = current state, memoir = founding).

## Corpus re-check (slice 09150316-13, 2026-09-15)
- Context absorbed: the NY merger was part of a broader North-American Taiwanese senior-association wave. The LA movement is documented in [[works/taiwaneseamericanhistory-org/ourjourneys54|54. 海外台灣人第一所老人公寓一鶴園 / 王桂榮 /10/2014]]: 台灣長輩會 was founded 1979-05-05 borrowing Los Angeles Gospel Church as its meeting place, first chair 陳夢蘭 (his pharmacist son 陳銓仁 as secretary); after a leadership dispute over re-election, 蕭華銓 led by the third term, the name was changed to 台灣老人會, an office with a recreation room was rented in Little Tokyo, and a monthly newsletter began; the narrator (then 48, an honorary member) mediated, donated ~US$1,000/year, and received the top-donor award at the 15th anniversary in 1994.
- Movement framing: [[works/taiwaneseamericanhistory-org/ourjourneys208|208. 台灣人社團的多元化與整合的必要性 / 出外人 /03/2016]] lists 各地台灣老人會 among the dozens of functional Taiwanese associations (alongside NATPA, TAA/台灣人公共事務協會, 台美人文藝協會, etc.) in a community of 200,000+ Taiwanese in North America — arguing diversification then consolidation, the exact pattern the NY three-association merger followed.
- Related senior-housing milestone in the same corpus: [[works/taiwaneseamericanhistory-org/ourjourneys173-eng|173. 台美人移民史的首座里程碑 / Flamingo Garden Senior Apartments / Fei-Kuang Tseng]].
