# Taiwanese Association of America in Greater St. Louis (聖路易)

## Identity Snapshot
- **English:** Taiwanese Association of America in Greater St. Louis
- **Chinese:** 聖路易
- **Era:** Contemporary
- **Geography:** Taiwanese American community
- **Core roles:** Community organization (TAH directory)

The Taiwanese Association of America in Greater St. Louis (TAA-STL; 聖路易 台灣同鄉會) is a regional chapter of the Taiwanese Association of America (TAA), the national umbrella organization for Taiwanese American community associations. The chapter serves the Taiwanese American community across the Greater St. Louis metropolitan area of Missouri.

## Role in the Community

- From Mu-Sheng Wu's TAA memoir ([[works/taiwaneseamericanhistory-org/ourjourneys76-eng|76. 全美台灣同鄉會 / Mu-Sheng Wu]], band A — primary material): St. Louis (聖路易) was one of the eight founding chapters of the National TACL (全國台美公民協會, alongside Houston, Kansas, Los Angeles, NYC, San Diego, San Francisco, Seattle) under 總會長 吳澧培, which threw full manpower and financial support (人力和財力) behind the Taiwanese-American census (人口普查) project; the campaign opened with a joint letter from twelve groups including TAA, TACL, FAPA, 人權會, NATMA, NATPA and NATWA. Note: TACL is a distinct organization from the TAA chapter, recorded here as adjacent community history.

- **City movement context (band A):** the St. Louis Taiwanese Christian community that overlapped the chapter is documented first-hand in 張理美's memoir — 1972 physician-family house meetings → 福爾摩斯基督徒圑契 (1978, registered by 張理美) → 1988 registration as 大聖路易台灣人長老基督教會, the first Taiwanese church in the US Midwest to join a mainstream US denomination (PCUSA 基底恩斯-拉芙究中會) → 1991-08-17 dedication of the first new church building built by Taiwanese Americans in the U.S. (Bovina; site-plan shaped as the character 「台」), which also served as the St. Louis Taiwanese gathering center — [[works/taiwaneseamericanhistory-org/ourjourneys305|305. 美國聖路易台灣人基督長老教會的歷史簡略 / 張理美]]; building record: [[works/taiwaneseamericanhistory-org/ff286|286. First New Church building by Taiwanese Americans in the U. S. / 1991]].
