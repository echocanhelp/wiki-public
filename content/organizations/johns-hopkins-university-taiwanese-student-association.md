# Johns Hopkins University Taiwanese Student Association

## Identity Snapshot
- **English:** Johns Hopkins University Taiwanese Student Association
- **Chinese:** —
- **Era:** Contemporary
- **Geography:** Taiwanese American community
- **Core roles:** Community organization (TAH directory)

The Johns Hopkins University Taiwanese Student Association (JHUTSA) is a student organization at Johns Hopkins University in Baltimore, Maryland. Its founding year is not documented in available first-party sources. JHUTSA primarily serves students from Taiwan and the broader Taiwanese diaspora, while also welcoming students of non-Taiwanese background who wish to engage with Taiwanese culture.

## Role in the Community
The corpus refers to the group as "Johns Hopkins TASA" (Taiwanese American Students Association) in the TaiwaneseAmerican.org record: in 2011, JHU student Alyssa Lee served as its president while leading the TaiwaneseAmerican.org "New Creatives" initiative, and the club ran a Taiwanese Night Market on campus that year ([[works/taiwaneseamerican-org/taiwanese-night-market-at-johns-hopkins-university|Taiwanese Night Market at Johns Hopkins University]]; a 2013 event is also recorded in [[works/taiwaneseamerican-org/night-market-at-johns-hopkins-university-in-md|Night Market at Johns Hopkins University in MD]]). The corpus preserves a second 2011 activity under the org's own byline form "jhuTASA": co-presenting a "Taste of Taiwan" event with JHU's International Audience Council on 2011-11-19 ([[works/taiwaneseamerican-org/jhutasa-and-iac-present-taste-of-taiwan|jhuTASA and IAC Present: Taste of Taiwan]]) — showing the club ran both food-market and intercultural-dialogue formats that year. Alyssa Lee's TASA presidency is also carried in the standing New Creatives bio on the initiative's 2022 work records ([[works/taiwaneseamerican-org/new-creatives-eric-lin-music|New Creatives: Eric Lin music]], [[works/taiwaneseamerican-org/rosalie-chiang-turning-red-interview|New Creatives: Rosalie Chiang interview]], [[works/taiwaneseamerican-org/oliver-lin-to-add-oil|New Creatives: Oliver Lin]]). In 2022, JHU biomedical-engineering sophomore Taliyah Huang — a second-generation Taiwanese American raised in a Taiwanese household — built the BobaWay English/Chinese-to-Taigi audio translation app while at Hopkins ([[works/taiwaneseamerican-org/taliyah-huang-interview-bobaway|Taliyah Huang interview: BobaWay]])). The corpus records another Taiwanese American JHU student of the same period: union organizer Bonnie Jin, who says her interest in Taiwan's youth politics (sparked by the 2014 Sunflower movement) grew "particularly while at Johns Hopkins University," before she went on to labor organizing with Mayor Michelle Wu's staff and the flight-attendants unionization campaign ([[works/taiwaneseamerican-org/bonnie-jin-union-organizer|Bonnie Jin: union organizer]]) — campus-community context only; her record does not state JHU student-association membership.
