# Johns Hopkins University Taiwanese Student Association

## Identity Snapshot
- **English:** Johns Hopkins University Taiwanese Student Association
- **Chinese:** —
- **Era:** Contemporary
- **Geography:** Taiwanese American community
- **Core roles:** Community organization (TAH directory)

The Johns Hopkins University Taiwanese Student Association (JHUTSA) is a student organization at Johns Hopkins University in Baltimore, Maryland. Its founding year is not documented in available first-party sources. JHUTSA primarily serves students from Taiwan and the broader Taiwanese diaspora, while also welcoming students of non-Taiwanese background who wish to engage with Taiwanese culture.

## Role in the Community
The corpus refers to the group as "Johns Hopkins TASA" (Taiwanese American Students Association) in the TaiwaneseAmerican.org record: in 2011, JHU student Alyssa Lee served as its president while leading the TaiwaneseAmerican.org "New Creatives" initiative, and the club ran a Taiwanese Night Market on campus that year ([[works/taiwaneseamerican-org/taiwanese-night-market-at-johns-hopkins-university|Taiwanese Night Market at Johns Hopkins University]]; a 2013 event is also recorded in [[works/taiwaneseamerican-org/night-market-at-johns-hopkins-university-in-md|Night Market at Johns Hopkins University in MD]]). In 2022, JHU biomedical-engineering sophomore Taliyah Huang — a second-generation Taiwanese American raised in a Taiwanese household — built the BobaWay English/Chinese-to-Taigi audio translation app while at Hopkins ([[works/taiwaneseamerican-org/taliyah-huang-interview-bobaway|Taliyah Huang interview: BobaWay]]).

The Baltimore campus sits in historic Taiwanese community territory: by the early 1960s Baltimore hosted a cluster of Taiwanese physicians training at the Johns Hopkins Medical Center (陳庵君、黃錫勳、洪朝煌、許日章、鄭敏雄 et al.) and Taiwanese graduate students (郭義雄、劉宜時), per the DC/Baltimore community memoir ([[works/taiwaneseamericanhistory-org/ourjourneys-138|Our Journeys 138]]) — social context predating the student association itself.
