# Harvard Taiwanese Cultural Society

## Identity Snapshot
- **English:** Harvard Taiwanese Cultural Society
- **Chinese:** 哈佛大學台灣文化學會
- **Era:** Contemporary
- **Geography:** Taiwanese American community
- **Core roles:** Community organization (TAH directory)

The Harvard Taiwanese Cultural Society (TCS) is a student-run organization at Harvard College dedicated to promoting the culture and heritage of Taiwan and Taiwanese Americans. Alumni records on the organization’s archived website document members as far back as the Class of 1995, placing the group’s founding in the early-to-mid 1990s, though an exact founding year is not confirmed in first-party sources. TCS describes its vision as fostering “a community in which people of all backgrounds can share, explore, and further develop an interest in Taiwanese culture,” and is recognized by Harvard College as one of the university’s student affinity groups.

## Role in the Community
- First-party directory name confirmed in the TAH corpus record #18 (2015-02-19): [[works/taiwaneseamericanhistory-org/harvard-taiwanese-cultural-society|TAH directory entry #18 哈佛大學台灣文化學會]].
- Alumni testimony documents TCS as a formative community anchor: Maggie Hsu (MochiMag interview) describes Harvard TCS's annual Winterfest — traditionally organized by four freshmen, feeding ~300 attendees with help from local "aunties" — as her first experience of large-scale project management: [[works/taiwaneseamerican-org/maggie-hsu-mochimag|Maggie Hsu — MochiMag]]. (The same interview notes parallel high-school and Stanford TCS chapters; those are separate campus groups, not this one.)
