# Columbia University Taiwanese American Students Association

## Identity Snapshot
- **English:** Columbia University Taiwanese American Students Association
- **Chinese:** —
- **Era:** Contemporary
- **Geography:** Taiwanese American community
- **Core roles:** Community organization (TAH directory)

The Columbia University Taiwanese American Students Association (CU TASA) is an undergraduate organization at Columbia University dedicated to building a community for individuals interested in Taiwanese culture. It is recognized as an official student group under Columbia College and Columbia Engineering’s Activities Board.

## Role in the Community

Columbia University has a long documented thread in the Taiwanese student movement in New York, predating CU TASA's current undergraduate form:

- **1969** — Radio Taiwan New York (紐約「臺灣之音」) director Chang Fu-hsiung (張富雄) recalled arriving at Columbia University as a student that year and seeing a dormitory sign, "Free China is neither free nor China," which set him on a path of searching for self-identity — a campus experience many overseas Taiwanese shared, per 何義麟's movement-history essay. See [[works/taiwaneseamericanhistory-org/our-journeys-357|357. 舊金山灣區臺灣人的社團活動與家國意識之形成]].
- **1991** — Columbia was a host campus for the founding-era ITASA "seeds" meeting: about twenty representatives from Harvard, Columbia, Yale, U Penn, Smith, Rutgers, and NYU converged on Columbia's campus for three days to shape the Intercollegiate Taiwanese American Students Association. This places a Columbia Taiwanese student group at the root of the intercollegiate network that present-day CU TASA inherits. See [[works/taiwaneseamerican-org/inspire-empower-and-activate-itasas-coming-of-age|Inspire, Empower, and Activate: ITASA's Coming of Age]] and [[works/taiwaneseamericanhistory-org/history-of-itasa|46. History of ITASA]].

HOLD: the TAH directory describes CU TASA as a contemporary undergraduate club; the corpus shows Taiwanese student organizing at Columbia from at least 1991 (ITASA seeds meeting). Whether today's CU TASA is a continuation of that earlier campus group or a later re-founding is unresolved — not auto-merged.
