# Presbyterian Church in Taiwan (PCT)

The **Presbyterian Church in Taiwan (PCT)** (Chinese: 台灣基督長老教會; Pe̍h-ōe-jī: Tâi-oân Ki-tok Tiúⁿ-ló Kàu-hōe) is the largest Protestant Christian denomination based in Taiwan. As of 2020, it comprises approximately 257,550 baptized members across roughly 1,271 congregations, representing a significant portion of the island's Christian population — particularly among indigenous peoples, where approximately 70% affiliate with the denomination.

The PCT is a member of the World Council of Churches, and its flag features a "burning bush," which signifies the concept of burning yet not being destroyed.

## Identity Snapshot

- **Type:** Christian denomination (Reformed/Presbyterian)
- **Geography:** Taiwan
- **Founded:** Mid-19th century (1865–1875)
- **Members:** ~257,550 (2020) across 1,271 congregations
- **Governance:** Presbyterian polity — 23 presbyteries, 4 districts, 1 General Assembly
- **Theology:** Reformed theology — Westminster Confession of Faith, Apostles' Creed, Nicene Creed, Heidelberg Catechism
- **Language:** Taiwanese Hokkien, Mandarin, indigenous languages
- **Official sites:** [pct.org.tw](https://www.pct.org.tw/) · [english.pct.org.tw](https://english.pct.org.tw/)
- **General Assembly office:** 3, Lane 269, Roosevelt Rd Sec. 3, Taipei 106 ([EN site footer](https://english.pct.org.tw/enWho_int_His.htm), archived 2026-08-18)
- **Tel:** +886-2-2362-5282 · **Fax:** +886-2-2362-8096 · pct@mail.pct.org.tw

## History

Official English “Who We Are → Our History – context and pluralism” ([enWho_int_His.htm](https://english.pct.org.tw/enWho_int_His.htm), archived 2026-08-18) is **island political context**, not a substitute for the mission timeline below.

PCT’s own wording: Dutch, Spanish, and Qing rule; Taiwan made a Qing province **1887**; ceded to Japan **1895** “in perpetuity”; fifty years of colonial development; **1945** Allied instruction that the KMT accept Japan’s surrender and occupy as trustee; **1949** KMT retreat to Taiwan; **228** (28 February 1947) “wiping out at least 20,000 elite Taiwanese leaders and youth”; martial law and a one-language policy until **1987**. About **3 million (13%)** “Mainlander” troops/civilians and descendants; later migrant workers (~300,000) and cross-cultural marriages.

Official “Our Isolation” ([enWho_int_Iso.htm](https://english.pct.org.tw/enWho_int_Iso.htm)): PCT prefers **self-determination** — Taiwan’s people choose their future; rejects forced “one China” unification.

**Church clock (mission):** **1865** Maxwell / Tainan · **1872** Mackay / Tamsui · **1951** single General Assembly. See Timeline. **Not** TPC 50週年 (FPCLA 1970).

## Historical Significance

The PCT has played a crucial role in:

1. **Education and healthcare** — pioneered Taiwan's first school, hospital, and printing press
2. **Social justice movements** — championed human rights, democracy, and indigenous rights
3. **Taiwanese identity and democratic development** — resisted assimilation policies, issued prophetic declarations
4. **Diaspora church connections** — maintains international partnerships worldwide
5. **Indigenous ministry** — approximately 70% of Taiwan's indigenous population affiliates with the PCT

## Timeline

- **1865** — James Laidlaw Maxwell arrives in Kaohsiung; establishes first Presbyterian congregation in Tainan (June 16)
- **1872** — George Leslie Mackay arrives in Tamsui; begins northern mission work
- **1885** — Taiwan Church News (Tâi-oân-hú-siâⁿ Kàu-hōe-pò) begins publication — Taiwan's first serialized printed newspaper
- **1895** — Treaty of Shimonoseki cedes Taiwan to Japan; PCT continues mission under colonial rule
- **1912** — Northern and southern presbyteries merge to form the Taiwan Synod of the Presbyterian Church (~30,000 worshippers)
- **1930s** — Intensified evangelism among indigenous communities begins
- **1945** — End of Japanese rule; retrocession to Republic of China
- **1951** — Northern and southern presbyteries unify into a single General Assembly
- **1955–1965** — Ten-Year Double the Church Movement: membership doubles from ~59,471 to ~118,942
- **1971** — December 29: "Statement on Our National Fate" issued, calling for self-determination and democratic reforms
- **1975** — "Our Appeal" published protesting confiscation of romanized Taiwanese and Atayal Bibles
- **1977** — August 16: "A Declaration on Human Rights" issued, demanding end to martial law and recognition of Taiwan's independence
- **1979** — December 10: Kaohsiung Incident; PCT General Secretary Rev. Kao Chun-ming arrested for sheltering dissident Shih Ming-teh
- **1980** — Kao sentenced to 7 years (served 4, released 1984); PCT rejoins World Council of Churches
- **1985** — PCT General Assembly adopts its own Confession of Faith (20 articles)
- **1987** — July 15: Martial law lifted; PCT's advocacy credited with contributing to democratization
- **1991** — August 20: "A Public Statement on the Sovereignty of Taiwan" reaffirms right to self-determination
- **1992** — "Anti-Nuclear Declaration" opposes nuclear energy on ethical and environmental grounds
- **2014** — Renewed mission agreement with Presbyterian Church (U.S.A.)
- **2025** — PCT celebrates 160th anniversary; WCC sends greetings

## Works

### Educational Institutions

The PCT pioneered modern education in Taiwan, establishing:

- **Taiwan Theological College and Seminary** (Taipei) — founded 1872, Taiwan's oldest theological school
- **Tainan Theological College and Seminary** — founded 1876 by missionary Thomas Barclay
- **Aletheia University** (Tamsui) — traces origins to George Leslie Mackay's Oxford College (1882)
- **Chang Jung Christian University** (Tainan) — established 1993, building on 1883 Presbyterian school foundations
- **Yushan Theological College and Seminary** — official related-institution list
- **Presbyterian Bible College**
- **Chang-Jung Girls' Senior High / Chang Jung Senior High / Tam-Kang Elementary–High** — official list
- **Mackay Nursing School · Mackay Medical College**

Official related-institution page also names **Taiwan Church Press** and **Mass Media Foundation** ([enWho_int_Ins.htm](https://english.pct.org.tw/enWho_int_Ins.htm)).

### Mission movement (official)

**One-leads-One New Doubling Movement** launched **2010**: Identity, Commitment, Growth; six dimensions through 2015 ([enWho_int_Eva.htm](https://english.pct.org.tw/enWho_int_Eva.htm)). Do not treat 2015 as a membership-count we have.

### Healthcare

- **Sin-lâu Hospital** (Tainan) — founded 1865 by James Laidlaw Maxwell; Taiwan's first modern Western-style medical facility
- **Mackay Memorial Hospital** — multiple branches across northern Taiwan, named after George Leslie Mackay
- **Changhua Christian Hospital** — affiliated through historical and ongoing cooperative ties

### Publishing

- **Taiwan Church Press** — established 1885; publishes Taiwan Church News, hymnals, biblical translations in Taiwanese and indigenous dialects
- **Exegetical Sermon Commentary Collection** — Old and New Testament volumes in traditional Chinese (since 2010)

## Governance

The PCT follows the presbyterian form of church government:

- **Local congregations** — governed by sessions (pastor + elected ruling elders)
- **23 presbyteries** — regional bodies handling ordination, pastoral oversight, mission coordination
- **4 districts** — administrative groupings of presbyteries
- **General Assembly** — highest legislative and judicial body; meets annually with equal lay and ordained commissioners
- **General Secretariat** — led by the General Secretary, handles executive functions between Assemblies

### Leadership

- **Moderator** — elected annually by the General Assembly; chairs sessions and represents the PCT externally
- **General Secretary** — leads the General Secretariat's executive staff
- **Associate General Secretaries** — handle specialized portfolios (church institutions, mission partnerships)

## Theology

The PCT subscribes to:

- **Apostles' Creed**
- **Nicene Creed**
- **Heidelberg Catechism**
- **Westminster Confession of Faith** (1646) — primary doctrinal standard
- **PCT Confession of Faith** (1985) — 20-article adaptation addressing Taiwan's historical and cultural realities. Official English translation (from the authorized Pe̍h-ōe-jī text of the **32nd General Assembly**) adopted by the GA Faith and Order Committee on **10 January 1986** ([enWho_con.htm](https://english.pct.org.tw/enWho_con.htm)).

The official confession names the church as **both universal and rooted in this land**.

### Logo

Official: burning bush — *Nec Tamen Consumebatur* — “burns but is not consumed.” “Since 1865 … scorched by the flames of repression … still stands” ([enWho_int_Log.htm](https://english.pct.org.tw/enWho_int_Log.htm)).

### Program committees (official EN list)

[enWho_int_Com.htm](https://english.pct.org.tw/enWho_int_Com.htm): Evangelism; Education; Church and Society; Ecumenical Relations; Youth; Campus; Women’s; Pine Life (seniors); Indigenous Mission; Hakka Mission; Ministers In-Service & Retirement Welfare; Finance; Law; Faith and Order; Liturgy and Music; Church History; Promote Taiwan’s Ethnic Languages; Higher Education; General Assembly Ministers; Gender Justice. **Names of sitting secretaries not on that page — not invented.**

### Core Beliefs

- **Trinitarian** — one true God as Creator, Redeemer, and Sustainer
- **Scripture** — Bible is inspired, infallible Word of God, supreme authority
- **Salvation** — by grace alone, through faith alone, in Christ alone
- **Sacraments** — Baptism and the Lord's Supper (two sacraments, not means of grace ex opere operato)
- **Predestination** — particular redemption, effectual calling, perseverance of the saints

## Political Engagement

### Advocacy for Human Rights and Democracy

The PCT has been a vocal proponent of human rights and democratic reforms, particularly during Taiwan's martial law period (1949–1987). The church's advocacy emphasized protections for speech, assembly, and indigenous rights, framing these as aligned with Christian principles of justice and human dignity.

### Key Public Statements and Declarations

### Conflicts with Authoritarian Regimes

The PCT confronted the KMT regime during martial law by publicly advocating for human rights, democratic reforms, and Taiwanese self-determination. Key events include:

- **1970** — PCT withdrew from WCC under KMT pressure (rejoined 1980)
- **1979** — Kaohsiung Incident; General Secretary Kao Chun-ming arrested and sentenced to 7 years
- **1979–1981** — KMT proposed legislation to regulate religious activities, viewing PCT advocacy as threat to national unity
- **1985** — "Confession of Faith" in romanized Taiwanese reinforced human rights and local identity

### Stance on National Identity and Geopolitics

The PCT maintains that Taiwan constitutes a sovereign nation with a distinct national identity rooted in self-determination. The church:

- Rejects incorporation into the People's Republic of China
- Supports Taiwan's de facto independence and international participation
- Condemned PRC military threats across the Taiwan Strait
- Rejected "one country, two systems" as incompatible with Taiwan's democratic values

## Institutional Contributions

### Educational Initiatives

The PCT has emphasized education as a core mission component, establishing Taiwan's first higher learning facilities, theological colleges, and universities. The church operates or supports high schools and middle schools, implementing Life Education curricula that integrate biblical principles into secular settings.

### Healthcare and Social Services

Beyond hospitals, the PCT extends social services via:

- **Fishermen's Service Centre** — vocational training and welfare support
- **Aboriginal Community Development Centre** — indigenous health and economic initiatives
- **Taiwan Women's Development Association** — gender-specific empowerment
- **Grace Home Cards program** — free medications and treatment at 106 partner clinics

### Media and Publishing

The Taiwan Church Press (established 1885) functions as the PCT's primary publishing entity. It initiated operations with vernacular Taiwanese printing in Pe̍h-ōe-jī romanization, facilitating early dissemination of Christian literature. The Taiwan Church News, the press's cornerstone periodical, debuted on March 7, 1885 — Taiwan's first serialized printed newspaper.

## Membership and Demographics

- **Total members:** ~257,550 (2020)
- **Congregations:** 1,271
- **Presbyteries:** 23
- **Indigenous membership:** ~70% of Taiwan's indigenous population (16 recognized tribes)
- **Growth peak:** 1955–1965 Ten-Year Double the Church Movement (doubled membership)
- **Current trend:** Stabilized or modest decline amid broader secularization
