# Taiwanese Student Association at Ohio State University (俄亥俄州立大學台灣同學會)

## Identity Snapshot
- **English:** Taiwanese Student Association at Ohio State University
- **Chinese:** 俄亥俄州立大學台灣同學會
- **Era:** Contemporary
- **Geography:** Taiwanese American community
- **Core roles:** Community organization (TAH directory)

The Taiwanese Student Association (TWSA) at The Ohio State University — known in Chinese as 俄亥俄州立大學台灣同學會 — is a student-led, service-oriented organization dedicated to supporting and connecting Taiwanese students on the Columbus campus. Its founding year is not documented on available first-party sources. The organization is primarily oriented toward students who have come from Taiwan to study at OSU, and all board members are actively enrolled OSU students.

## Role in the Community
- Community memoirs record an Ohio State University Taiwanese Association as one of the two most typical examples (alongside UT Austin's) of the wave of campus 台灣人社 that "sprouted up like bamboo shoots after a spring rain" around 1970 — founded by students who broke away from KMT-agent-monitored campus gatherings. See [[works/taiwaneseamericanhistory-org/ourjourneys76-eng|Our Journeys #76]].
- An Ohio State 大學 team fielded in the 1st Midwest Taiwanese Softball Tournament, 1973-06-16/17 at Purdue (11 teams). See [[works/taiwaneseamericanhistory-org/our-journeys-376|Our Journeys #376]]; the same memoir notes a Columbus 鄉訊 newsletter circulated in the Midwest community by the late 1980s.
- The current-era TWSA has its own TAH directory record in the corpus: [[works/taiwaneseamericanhistory-org/osu-taiwanese-student-association|OSU Taiwanese Student Association (TAH directory)]]. OSU also hosted the 2013 ITASA Midwest Conference — [[works/taiwaneseamerican-org/2013-itasa-midwest-conference-at-the-ohio-state-university|2013 ITASA Midwest Conference at OSU]] — evidence of an active Taiwanese student organization on campus in the modern era.
- HOLD: the 1970s movement-era OSU 台灣人社 vs the current service-oriented TWSA (founding year undocumented) — continuity unverified; the 2013 ITASA conference confirms modern-era activity but does not close the 1970s→present lineage question.
