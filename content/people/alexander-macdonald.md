# James Alexander Macdonald (1862–1923)

Editor, Presbyterian minister, school principal, orator, social reformer, and author. He is the
link between this page's old redirect slug and George Leslie Mackay: Macdonald edited the 1896
biography of Mackay, *From far Formosa…*, which is why this file was historically a mix of
Mackay + Maxwell + Macdonald material.

## Life (Dictionary of Canadian Biography)

- Born 22 Jan. 1862 in East Williams Township, Upper Canada, son of John Alexander Macdonald, a
  farmer, and Jane Grant; died 14 May 1923 in Toronto. Source: Dictionary of Canadian Biography,
  entry "MACDONALD, JAMES ALEXANDER" (biographer Brian J. Fraser) — cached at
  `sources/dcb-james-alexander-macdonald-2026-11-12.md`.
- Married 11 June 1890 Grace Lumsden Christian in Oil City, Ont.; they had two sons and a daughter.
  Source: `sources/dcb-james-alexander-macdonald-2026-11-12.md`.
- Traced his ancestry to Glen Urquhart in the Highlands of Scotland; his great-great-grandfather
  survived the battle of Culloden in 1746 and fought for the British in the American revolution.
  Source: `sources/dcb-james-alexander-macdonald-2026-11-12.md`.
- Entered the University of Toronto in 1878; studied at Knox College in Toronto from 1883 to 1887,
  where principal William Caven was a lasting mentor. Source:
  `sources/dcb-james-alexander-macdonald-2026-11-12.md`.
- Joined the editorial staff of the *Knox College Monthly* in 1885 and led it until 1892; graduated
  in 1887 and won the Fischer Scholarship in systematic theology. Source:
  `sources/dcb-james-alexander-macdonald-2026-11-12.md`.
- Ordained in 1891 and appointed to Knox Presbyterian Church in St Thomas, Ont. Source:
  `sources/dcb-james-alexander-macdonald-2026-11-12.md`.
- Edited *From far Formosa…* (Toronto, 1896), the biography of missionary George Leslie Mackay.
  Source: `sources/dcb-james-alexander-macdonald-2026-11-12.md`; see canonical page
  [[people/george-leslie-mackay|George Leslie Mackay (馬偕)]].
- In 1896 returned to Toronto as principal of the Presbyterian Ladies' College (five years) and
  editor of the new Presbyterian monthly *Westminster*. Source:
  `sources/dcb-james-alexander-macdonald-2026-11-12.md`.
- As managing editor of the *Globe* (1903–15) attacked the Ross government's corruption ("The
  barnacles on the ship must be treated with an iron hand", 9 Nov. 1903) and won acquittal in 1910
  of the libel suit brought by MP George Eulas Foster. Source:
  `sources/dcb-james-alexander-macdonald-2026-11-12.md`.
- Received honorary doctorates from the universities of Glasgow (1909), Edinburgh (1911), and
  Oberlin College (1915); appointed to the University of Toronto board of governors in 1906.
  Source: `sources/dcb-james-alexander-macdonald-2026-11-12.md`.
- Left the *Globe* suddenly on 24 Nov. 1915 after controversy over his peace rallies in the United
  States, including Henry Ford's "spontaneous" gift of a motor car after his Detroit address in
  April 1915. Source: `sources/dcb-james-alexander-macdonald-2026-11-12.md`.
- Delivered the Cole Lectures at Vanderbilt University in 1917, published as *The North American
  idea*; breakdowns in 1917 forced his retirement and he died in 1923. Source:
  `sources/dcb-james-alexander-macdonald-2026-11-12.md`.

## Why linked

The old redirect claimed this person was George Leslie Mackay; he was not. James Alexander
Macdonald was the editor of Mackay's 1896 biography *From far Formosa* and the most influential
Presbyterian clergyman of his generation in Canada — a distinct person whose own page the corpus
had lost.
