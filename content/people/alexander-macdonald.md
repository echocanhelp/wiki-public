# 馬偕博士 (George Leslie Macdonald)

**George Leslie Macdonald** (馬偕, 1838–1901), commonly known in Chinese as **馬偕博士** (Dr. Maxwell), was a Canadian Presbyterian missionary, medical doctor, evangelist, and educator who spent the last 29 years of his life in northern Taiwan, profoundly shaping its religious, educational, and medical landscape.

## Identity Snapshot

- **Full name:** George Leslie Macdonald (馬偕)
- **Born:** September 21, 1838, Tullydonnell, Ontario, Canada
- **Died:** June 2, 1901, Tamsui, Taiwan (aged 62)
- **Education:** Roxbury Latin School; Toronto University (MD); Toronto Missionary College
- **Marriage:** Married Henrietta Elizabeth Runnels in 1872; they had one daughter, Emily
- **Role:** Presbyterian medical missionary, evangelist, educator, physician
- **Mission field:** Northern Taiwan (1872–1901)
- **Known as:** 馬偕 (Ma Kai) in Chinese; 馬偕博士 (Dr. Maxwell) by the Taiwanese people
- **Legacy:** Founded the Presbyterian Church in Northern Taiwan, 偕醫學堂 (Ochong Medical School), and the Tamsui Theological College (now STM Taiwan)

## Life and Mission

### Arrival in Taiwan

George Leslie Macdonald arrived in Tamsui (淡水), northern Taiwan, on August 23, 1872, after studying theology and medicine in Canada and the United States. He was ordained by the Canadian Presbyterian Church and dispatched as a missionary to Taiwan. At age 34, he began his work in a region that was largely unfamiliar to Westerners.

### Medical Work

Macdonald established the first Western medicine clinic in northern Taiwan, providing free medical care to the local population. He treated patients from all social backgrounds, often traveling on foot or by horse to reach remote villages. His medical work was instrumental in gaining the trust of the local community and served as a gateway for his evangelistic efforts.

### Educational Contributions

His educational legacy is perhaps his most enduring contribution:

- **偕醫學堂 (Ochong Medical School, 1880):** Founded the first modern medical school in northern Taiwan, training local physicians in Western medicine. The school later became part of the National Taiwan University system.
- **Tamsui Theological College (1881):** Established to train Taiwanese pastors and evangelists, ensuring the sustainability of the church. This institution evolved into the current **STM Taiwan** (聖約翰大學 / Taiwan Theological College and Seminary).
- **Girls' Education:** Macdonald was a pioneer in advocating for girls' education in 19th-century Taiwan, establishing schools for girls at a time when this was highly unconventional.

### Evangelism

Macdonald walked an estimated 87,500 kilometers across northern Taiwan during his 29-year mission. He:

- Established over 60 churches across northern Taiwan
- Baptized more than 1,700 converts
- Trained and ordained numerous Taiwanese pastors who continued his work after his death
- Used the local language (Hokkien) in his preaching, learning it within months of arrival

### Language and Cultural Work

Macdonald developed a romanization system for the Hokkien language (known as the "Macdonald system" or 馬唐字) to facilitate Bible translation and local preaching. He also wrote a Hokkien dictionary and grammar, contributing to linguistic scholarship.

## Death and Legacy

馬偕博士 died on June 2, 1901, in Tamsui, Taiwan, from a leg infection that he contracted while visiting a patient in a remote area. Despite his deteriorating health, he continued his work until his final days. He was buried in the church cemetery in Tamsui, where his grave remains a historical site.

His death deeply affected the Taiwanese Christian community. According to historical accounts, thousands of Taiwanese people — both Christian and non-Christian — mourned his passing, testifying to the depth of his impact on the region.

### Institutions Carrying His Legacy

- **偕醫中學 (Ochong Teachers College):** Now part of the National Taiwan University system
- **STM Taiwan (台灣神學院):** Direct descendant of the Tamsui Theological College he founded
- **馬偕紀念醫院 (Mackay Memorial Hospital):** One of Taiwan's largest hospital networks, named in his honor
- **馬偕紀念醫院宜蘭分院:** Branch hospital continuing his medical mission
- **真理大學 (Tunghais University):** Has connections to his educational legacy

### Commemoration

馬偕博士's 150th anniversary of missionary work in Taiwan was commemorated in 2022, including special worship services such as the 馬偕博士台灣宣教150週年紀念主日 hosted by [[organizations/good-shepherd-taiwanese-presbyterian-church|Good Shepherd Taiwanese Presbyterian Church]] on March 6, 2022.

His legacy continues through the many institutions he founded, including STM Taiwan (where [[people/mingyuan-hsu|Rev. Ming Yuan Hsu]] studied), the Presbyterian Church in Taiwan, and Mackay Memorial Hospital.
