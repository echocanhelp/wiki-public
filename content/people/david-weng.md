## Role in the Community

Community memoirs record Weng as a **founding force behind the Taiwanese American Center of Northern California (北加州台灣會館)** — Northern California's first public Taiwanese American gathering place ("公厝"). He is named among the like-minded friends invited by 張信行 and 陳德輝 who founded the Fremont center in March 2003 ([[works/taiwaneseamericanhistory-org/ourjourneys53-eng|TAH #53: Our Journeys (EN)]]; ZH: [[works/taiwaneseamericanhistory-org/ourjourneys53|Our Journeys]]), and again in the Center's preparation-committee roster in 張信行's account of the 2003 founding ([[works/taiwaneseamericanhistory-org/ourjourneys24-eng|Our Journeys (EN)]], [[works/taiwaneseamericanhistory-org/ourjourneys24|ourjourneys24]]; ZH committee record: [[works/taiwaneseamericanhistory-org/ourjourneys38|ourjourneys38]]).

The same memoirs credit him with the Center's permanent-home campaign: together with Chun Chiu (邱俊邦) he learned that Canaan Church was outgrowing its building, which sparked the plan to share a large property; Weng led the site searches — repeatedly inviting 陳德輝, 張信行 and the author to inspect locations — that culminated in the March 2009 move to the 8,400 sq ft San Jose Golden Triangle location ([[works/taiwaneseamericanhistory-org/ourjourneys53-eng|TAH #53]]).

His own encyclopedia record is preserved at [[works/taiwaneseamericanhistory-org/who-who-1870-david-weng|TAH #1870: David Weng 翁嘉盛]].
