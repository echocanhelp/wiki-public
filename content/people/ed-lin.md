## Role in the Community

- First author to win three Asian American Literary Awards: Waylaid (Kaya Press, 2002) and This Is a Bust (2007) each won the Members' Choice Award, and Snakes Can't Run (Minotaur Books, 2010) also won. Waylaid was adapted into the film The Motel by Michael Kang.
- Robert Chow series — Chinese-American NYPD detective novels set in 1976 New York (Snakes Can't Run, One Red Bastard, 2012) — grew from his research into Chinese-American history in 1976; see [[works/taiwaneseamerican-org/streetside-chat-with-author-ed-lin|2012 West Coast tour chat]] (recorded by Ho Chie Tsai at Eastwind Books, Berkeley).
- Turned to Taiwan as subject matter: Ghost Month (Soho Crime, 2014), a Taipei-set mystery whose protagonist Jing-nan works at Shilin Night Market, and the Taiwan-based novel Incensed (2016 tour) — [[works/taiwaneseamerican-org/ed-lin-interview|2014 interview with Anna Wu]], [[works/taiwaneseamerican-org/author-ed-lin-on-tour-with-new-taiwan-based-novel-incensed|2016 Incensed tour record]]. In the interview he discusses his family (father's family in Taiwan since shortly after the fall of the Ming Dynasty in the 1600s; mother a waishengren from Northern China who came to favor Taiwanese independence) and says Taiwan's low international profile is a core motivation for his writing.
