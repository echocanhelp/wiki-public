## Role in the Community
- Long-documented figure in Taiwanese American community media. Ho Chie Tsai's 2008 interview records his UCLA Film/Theatre/TV, Goldsmiths (London) and Carter Thor Studio training and early credits — Popular, The Shield, Without a Trace, ER, Studio 60, Grandma's Boy, Elizabethtown, Milk ([[works/taiwaneseamerican-org/a-chat-with-actor-kelvin-yu|A Chat with Actor Kelvin Yu]], 2008-11-19)).
- 2016 community interview at his Bento Box studio: played Taiwanese American Brian Cheng on Netflix's Master of None (character shaped by TA producer Alan Yang); writer/producer on FOX's Bob's Burgers (《開心漢堡店》), which won the 2014 Primetime Emmy for Outstanding Animated Program; in 2016 won an Annie Award for Outstanding Achievement in Writing in an Animated TV/Broadcast Production with co-writer Steven Davis ([[works/taiwaneseamerican-org/kelvin-yu|Meet Jack of All Trades: Actor, Writer, Producer Kelvin Yu]], 2016-02-18)).

In the Studio with Actor Kelvin Yu]], 2016-01-08)); Hollywood-diversity feature ([[works/taiwaneseamericanhistory-org/1-kelvin-yu-on-diversity-hollywood-and-how-master-of-none-gets-it-right|1. - The 2020-11-19 TJJ note's 《開心漢堡店》 Emmy claim matches the corpus's 2014 Bob's Burgers Emmy win — reconciled, no conflict.
- Produced the Disney+ adaptation of *American Born Chinese* (per corpus feature, 2023-05-24): recorded as a Taiwanese American producer on the show and **former TAF camper (Taiwanese American Foundation)** — tying his media career back to the community pipeline; the first episode was co-written by Alan Yang, Kelvin Yu, and his brother Charles Yu, and dedicated in memory of their grandmother Betty Yu — [[works/taiwaneseamerican-org/american-born-chinese-disney|The Roots of Disney's "American Born Chinese"]], 2023-05-24.
- Community-education note from the same record: the Betty L. Yu and Jin C. Yu Creative Writing Prize (established 2021 by Charles Yu) honors Betty Lin Yu and Jin-Chyuan Yu's service to the TA community — TACL LID Youth Camp (SoCal), co-founding the South Bay Taiwanese-American School (recorded as the first U.S. school specifically for Taiwanese-language instruction), NATEA-SC, and support for FAPA/NATWA/TAA.
