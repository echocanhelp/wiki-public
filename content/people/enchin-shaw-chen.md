## Pioneer Record & Community Founding
- The TAH encyclopedia entry #343 records her as **the first female Taiwanese student studying in the U.S.**, under the heading 「The first Female Taiwanese Student Studying in the U.S.A. / November 1, 1953」 — the earliest dated record of her U.S. studies in the vault. Entries #292 and #291 record her as a founding member of the **Formosan Club of Washington D.C.**, which she documented by printing **the club's first directory in 1966**, and of the **Taiwanese Presbyterian Church of Washington D.C.**, which was **started at her house in 1972** — consistent with her employment record as founder of the Washington church.
- Career in social work and mental health in the U.S.: Director of Social Service at Springfield Psychiatric Hospital (1957–1964) and at Edgemeade School (1964–1968), after a B.S. in Psychology at San Diego State College (1957) and an M.S. in Social Work at the University of Pennsylvania (1960).
- Founding Head of the Rongshing Children's Choir (1996–1999), extending her service from social work into children's musical formation.
- Author of the family history book 《蕭安居牧師生平及家譜》 *Life And Family History Of Rev. An-Ku Shaw* (03/2008), preserving the record of the next generation's ministerial lineage in the [[organizations/presbyterian-church-in-taiwan||Presbyterian Church in Taiwan]] milieu.
- **Church lineage:** the 華府基督長老教會 thirty-year commemorative issue records her father **Rev. L.S. Shaw (蕭樂善牧師)** as the church's **first pastor** ([[works/taiwaneseamericanhistory-org/mystories599|TAH #599]]); she is also the granddaughter of **Rev. An-Ku Shaw (蕭安居牧師)**, whose life her 2008 family history book preserves ([[works/taiwaneseamericanhistory-org/942-life-and-family-history-of-rev-an-ku-shaw|TAH #942]]).
- **Church founding narrative:** her own account "The Beginning of Our Church" documents the origin of the Washington church founded by her circle ([[works/taiwaneseamericanhistory-org/ourjourneys215|TAH #215]]).
- **Early-education record:** 朱真一's survey of early postwar Taiwanese women who studied in North America records her 1930 birth, 1952 graduation from 淡江英專, 1953 departure for the U.S., and 1965 marriage to **Dr. Donald C. J. Chen (陳哲仁)**, the D.C. area's first Taiwanese private-practice physician ([[works/taiwaneseamericanhistory-org/ourjourneys270|TAH #270]]; see also [[works/taiwaneseamericanhistory-org/whos474-donald-c-j-chen|TAH #474]] and [[works/taiwaneseamericanhistory-org/ff309|TAH #309]] on her husband's clinic).
- **Family-network context:** her husband Dr. Chen led the Washington D.C. branch among the 28 founding chapters of the **Formosan Club of America (全美台灣同鄉會)**, registered in D.C. in 1971 ([[works/taiwaneseamericanhistory-org/ourjourneys76|TAH #76]]; [[people/donald-c-j-chen|Donald C.J. Chen]]). Family photographs are held in the vault album of the Chen household ([[works/taiwaneseamericanhistory-org/photo-albums-activities-111|TAH #111]]).

## Timeline

Absorbed from on-page records (TAH #343, #292, #291, #473, #270, #599; no external sources):

- 1930 — born in Taiwan; granddaughter of Rev. An-Ku Shaw (蕭安居牧師) ([[works/taiwaneseamericanhistory-org/ourjourneys270|TAH #270]]).
- 1952 — graduated from 淡江英專 ([[works/taiwaneseamericanhistory-org/ourjourneys270|TAH #270]]).
- 1953-11-01 — recorded as the first female Taiwanese student studying in the U.S. ([TAH #343](https://taiwaneseamericanhistory.org/ff343/); corroborated in [[works/taiwaneseamericanhistory-org/mystories599|TAH #599]]).
- 1957 — B.S. Psychology, San Diego State College; began as Director of Social Service, Springfield Psychiatric Hospital (1957–1964).
- 1960 — M.S. Social Work, University of Pennsylvania.
- 1964–1968 — Director of Social Service, Edgemeade School.
- 1965 — married Dr. Donald C. J. Chen (陳哲仁), ENT specialist in the D.C. area ([[works/taiwaneseamericanhistory-org/ourjourneys270|TAH #270]]; [[people/donald-c-j-chen|Donald C.J. Chen]]).
- 1966 — printed the first directory of the Formosan Club of Washington D.C., of which she was a founding member ([[works/taiwaneseamericanhistory-org/ff292|TAH #292]]).
- 1971 — Formosan Club of America (全美台灣同鄉會) registered in Washington D.C. with 28 founding chapters; her husband's name heads the D.C. chapter list ([[works/taiwaneseamericanhistory-org/ourjourneys76|TAH #76]]).
- 1972 — Taiwanese Presbyterian Church of Washington D.C. started at her house; she was a founding member ([[works/taiwaneseamericanhistory-org/ff291|TAH #291]]; her own account in [[works/taiwaneseamericanhistory-org/ourjourneys215|TAH #215]]). Her father Rev. L.S. Shaw (蕭樂善牧師) is recorded as the church's first pastor ([[works/taiwaneseamericanhistory-org/mystories599|TAH #599]]).
- 1996–1999 — Founding Head, Rongshing Children's Choir.
- 2008-03 — published the family history book 《蕭安居牧師生平及家譜》 *Life And Family History Of Rev. An-Ku Shaw*, preserving the record of the [[organizations/presbyterian-church-in-taiwan|Presbyterian Church in Taiwan]] milieu ([[works/taiwaneseamericanhistory-org/942-life-and-family-history-of-rev-an-ku-shaw|TAH #942]]).
