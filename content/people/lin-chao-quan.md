# Lin Chao-quan (林朝權)

**Lin Chao-quan** (林朝權, 1900s–1980s) was a Taiwanese sports leader and democratic activist, best known as the second cousin (二舅/二叔) of biochemist 廖述宗 (Liao Shu-zong). He was a close friend of 孫文 (Sun Yat-sen) and served as a 國民大會代表 (National Assembly representative).

## Political Activism

Lin Chao-quan was born in 清水 (Qingshui), the same hometown as the Yang family. His political consciousness was shaped early by witnessing the 二二八 (228) Incident, where he saw the Nationalist government's massacre of Taiwanese people. This experience led him to develop anti-KMT sentiment and a belief that Taiwanese should forge their own path rather than认中國為祖國 (recognize China as the motherland).

Rather than fleeing to Japan, Lin went to Hong Kong and then entered China. Because of his advocacy for Taiwan independence, he was arrested by the Communist government and imprisoned for 25 years.

## Post-War Sports Leadership

After World War II (終戰), Lin Chao-quan became a leading figure in Taiwan's sports community. He held the position of 國民大會代表 (National Assembly representative), though he later became disillusioned with the KMT government's corruption.

## 1984 LA Olympics Visit

In 1984, during the Los Angeles Olympics, Lin Chao-quan visited Los Angeles. He was visited by 楊嘉猷 (Charles Yang), who brought him three books: "江文也傳" (Biography of Wen-yeh Chiang), [[people/peng-ming-min||彭明敏]]'s "自由的滋味" (The Taste of Freedom), and 吳濁流's "無花果" (Figless Fruit), hoping to help him reconnect with his Taiwanese identity.
