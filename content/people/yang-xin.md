# Yang Hsin (楊信)

**Yang Hsin** (楊信, born 1944), also known in English as **Jackson Yang**, is a Taiwanese American entrepreneur and community leader. He is the CEO of **Seville Classics, Inc.**, a globally leading innovator and manufacturer in housewares and storage, generating annual revenue of over $200 million. Seville was awarded "Vendor of the Year" by Walmart. He is also the Chairman of **FGB** (First Guangfu Bank), a community bank focused on serving small businesses and individuals in Southern California.

## Early Life

Yang Hsin was born in 1944 in Puxin Township, Changhua County — the year before the end of World War II. His family had 7 siblings; he was the youngest and most pampered. His mother never attended school but was a hardworking rural woman who managed the household with great frugality.

When Yang was in 6th grade, his father died suddenly. The family lost its center of gravity. His mother managed alone, borrowing money from neighbors to pay his school registration fees. She rented out the family's small plot of farmland and sold the rice harvest to pay debts. Yang's meals were mostly sweet potatoes with a little white rice — he was too embarrassed to open his lunchbox at school because most of it was sweet potato.

His first real memory of white rice was at a classmate's temple festival, where he was invited to stay overnight and given a white-rice lunchbox. He still remembers that feeling vividly.

He passed the exam for middle school but attended only one semester before dropping out. He could not bear to see his mother borrowing from neighbors every semester for his fees, and decided to become self-reliant.

### First Job: Soy Sauce Factory

His first job was at an uncle's soy sauce factory in Yunlin, where his main task was washing recycled old soy sauce bottles. The bottles were returned by villagers and were very dirty — they had to be soaked overnight in water, then scrubbed to remove soy sauce residue.

That winter was particularly cold. Yang kept his hands in cold water all day, and they became severely frostbitten — swollen, blistered, and rotting. When he returned home for Chinese New Year, his mother saw his hands and could not bear it. She decided to send him to Taipei to learn trade instead.

### Taipei Trade Company

In 1961, at age 17, Yang took a slow train from Yuanlin Station to Taipei — a 5-hour journey standing room only, since it was just after Chinese New Year and the trains were packed.

In Taipei, he worked at a small international trade company on Yanping North Road. The company had only two people: the owner and Yang. His job was to draft English product introduction letters. He also opened and checked the company's mailbox at the Post Office every morning and evening — international trade at that time used postal boxes as the primary communication address.

The owner's wife helped another company with bookkeeping. Yang learned the importance of English and, despite his meager salary of 80 NTD per month (less than the 200-300 NTD evening class fee), enrolled at "Melton" (美爾頓), an English cram school.

After nearly 4 years, Yang advanced from a junior clerk to a "small adult" (junior staff). The company specialized in canned food exports — pineapple, orange, lychee, mushroom, bamboo shoot, and asparagus — mainly to Germany and the United States. Taiwanese countryside mushrooms had low production costs, and the profit margin was excellent: about $10+ per box. The company made a lot of money.

## Military Service and Independent Business

After military service, Yang's friend Huang Shicheng (黃石城, later County Magistrate of Changhua) encouraged him to start his own trade company. Yang had no capital, so he organized a "rotating savings association" (招會仔) — 20 people contributing 500 NTD each, totaling 10,000 NTD. With this money, he bought an Underwood typewriter, rented a small office on Chongqing South Road, and began his international trade career.

### Immigration to the United States

In 1984, Yang and his wife He Qiaoling visited the United States for summer vacation. Their children loved America so much they said they didn't want to return to Taiwan. The children's longing for America, combined with his wife's "keen insight" about the US, led to a firm decision to immigrate.

Yang's wife, 何巧玲, first brought the 4 children to the US. Yang remained in Taiwan for two years as an "air traveler" (空中飛人), gradually shifting his business center to America.

In 1986, Yang joined his family in the US and settled down, starting a new chapter of his career.

## Building Seville Classics

### The Swap Meet Years

When Yang first arrived in America, everything was unfamiliar. Fortunately, his good friend Mr. Lin Jilong (林基龍) and his wife helped them find housing and settle in.

Yang discovered that his company in America, run by a manager, had unsold products堆积 (piling up) in the warehouse. He had to sell them himself — loading products onto his truck and taking them to swap meets (跳蚤市場) every weekend.

He quickly discovered that not all products sold well — there was a gap between supply and demand. "What should I sell?" was the key question. Yang spent two years at swap meets, learning American consumer preferences. He also frequently traveled to Taiwan and China to select products to test the US market.

### The Seat Cushion Breakthrough

At a Guangzhou trade fair in China, Yang saw a wooden bead seat cushion — the kind taxi drivers in Taiwan used. He thought: "America has so many cars, but no similar product. This is worth trying." He bought 2,000 seat cushions for over $10,000.

When he brought them to his American sales representative, he was met with cold rejection: "Americans won't buy this."

Yang was discouraged but had no choice — the goods were already shipped. He decided to sell them at the swap meet himself to test the market.

He bought two used car seats from an old car lot, placed the wooden bead cushions on them, and put round bead cushions on top, selling them with the selling points of "cooling" and "massage." Customers could sit and feel the product firsthand.

The result: the cushions sold incredibly well — dozens per day. Each cushion cost $5 to make, and he sold them for $15.

### Comfortbeads and Global Scale

Yang named the product **"Comfortbeads" (舒適珠)** and packaged them in beautifully designed hard boxes with pictures showing the product installed on car seats. He priced them at $12.99.

He approached BJ's Wholesale Club, a home goods chain with over a dozen stores on the East Coast. He negotiated a "two-week trial" — if the product didn't sell, he would take it back unconditionally.

Two weeks after shipping, Yang received a call: the client wanted immediate restocking because Comfortbeeds sold so well, even surprising the store. Yang immediately bought a ticket to China, found the manufacturer, and ordered over 7,000 units. The first shipping container was imported in bulk. Packaging boxes were printed in Taiwan and assembled/shipped from Los Angeles.

After the product became popular, Price Club (the predecessor of Costco) and Walmart also began selling it, opening the entire US big-box store market.

### Quality Crisis and Recovery

Not all experiences were smooth. Each seat cushion had over 500 wooden beads strung together with nylon thread by hand. The Chinese-made nylon thread was not strong enough — American customers were larger, and when they sat on the cushions, the thread broke and beads scattered, leading to high return rates that damaged his reputation.

Yang rushed back to Taiwan, found high-quality, strong beef tendon thread, and had it used on the production line. This improved product quality, reduced defect rates, and consolidated his reputation.

At peak, he imported over 200,000 units per week from China. The large volume drove costs down — each unit cost as little as $1.50. He sold to stores for $7-8, and they retailed at $9.99. In 1988-1990, $9.99 was an incredibly cheap product for American consumers, so it sold fast.

**Comfortbeads sold 10 million units across the US in 3 years**, earning Yang his "first pot of gold" in America and laying the foundation for his business.

### Seville Classics, Inc.

Over the years, Yang built Seville Classics, Inc. into a globally leading innovator and manufacturer in housewares and storage. The company generates annual revenue of over $200 million and was awarded "Vendor of the Year" by Walmart.

## Banking and Community Leadership

### FGB Community Bank

Yang is the Chairman of **FGB** (First Guangfu Bank), a community bank mainly focusing on serving small businesses and individuals in Southern California. In 2012, FGB ranked first among the nation's best forming community banks by SNL Financial. As of December 31, 2016, FGB's total assets exceeded $840 million, operating 4 branches across the Greater San Gabriel Valley and Orange County.

### Chamber of Commerce Leadership

- **Los Angeles Chinese American Chamber of Commerce:** President (2004)
- **Taiwanese Chambers of Commerce of North America:** President (initiated "Second Generation Assistance" program to introduce young Taiwanese Americans to the real world)
- **World Taiwanese Chambers of Commerce (WTCC):** President (2013–2014)
- **World United Council of Chinese Chambers of Commerce:** 20th President (2006)

### WTCC Presidency

Yang's WTCC presidency was marked by grand events:
- **2006 (19th WTCC):** Hosted in Hawaii with help from the Hawaiian Chinese Chamber of Commerce. Despite the high cost, Yang covered all expenses. The event coincided with Halloween, and Yang chartered a boat for a sea celebration. Attendees found it unforgettable.
- **2007:** Second session in Dallas; annual meeting scheduled for 2008 in Los Angeles.
- **2008 (20th WTCC):** Hosted in Los Angeles. Yang organized a three-day, two-night Mexico cruise specifically offering discounted rates for second-generation youth members. Many young members joined, and several couples met on the cruise. Yang served as their wedding witness — a story still told in the community. He was frequently asked to organize similar cruises.
- **2013 (19th WTCC):** Hosted at the Grand Hyatt in Taipei. Yang invested $70,000-80,000 to upgrade the stage per the requirements of popular Taiwanese rock band "Power Station" (動力火車), producing a spectacular evening where attendees waved electronic glow sticks. The event was described as "high to the sky."

### TPP Lobbying Trip

One of Yang's most significant initiatives as WTCC President was the **"Washington DC TPP Knock-on-Door Tour" (華府 TPP 叩門之旅)**.

Understanding the importance of the Trans-Pacific Partnership (TPP) agreement for Taiwan and US shared interests, Yang broke precedent by leading a delegation to Washington DC to meet with US Congress members and officials — instead of the traditional Beijing visits that previous WTCC Presidents had conducted.

The proposal received enthusiastic support from the Overseas Community Affairs Council (僑務委員會), Ministry of Foreign Affairs, Taipei Economic and Cultural Office in Los Angeles, and Washington office.

Thanks to Taiwanese Americans' strong support for local Congress members, Yang's delegation had deep relationships. Lawyer Hu Weigang (胡維剛) and his wife provided significant assistance.

Upon arrival at Capitol Hill, House Foreign Affairs Committee Chairman **Ed Royce** hosted a welcome reception in the House chamber for the delegation, with nearly 30 Congress members attending — a grand and impressive event.

## Personal Philosophy

Yang credits his life journey to three sources of help:
1. **His mother's love** — sent him to Taipei to learn trade
2. **His wife's vision** — decided to move to America to open new horizons
3. **A friend's help** — "when far from home, a friend makes all the difference"

He describes his life as having always been blessed by "noble people" (貴人) who helped him at critical moments.
