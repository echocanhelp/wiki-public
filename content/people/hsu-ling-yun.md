# Hsu Ling-yun (許凌雲)

**Hsu Ling-yun** (許凌雲; Tâi-lô: **Khóo Lîng-hûn**; given name 漢厚, courtesy name 凌雲 — he is known by the courtesy name — style 卓夫; 1862 – 14 September 1944) was a Qing *shengyuan* (秀才) from **下瓦硐** (瓦硐港下社), Baisha, Penghu; a Han-learning teacher; and an early Presbyterian elder. Locals call him the **Watong xiucai** (瓦硐秀才). The *續修澎湖縣志* treats him as **Penghu’s last Qing xiucai**. His former home at Watong Village No. 51 is the [[organizations/hsu-ling-yun-xiucai-memorial-hall|Hsu Ling-yun Xiucai Memorial Hall]].

Yang Shih-yang’s *信仰偉人列傳* once listed him as a *jinshi* in 1891; later work (Hsu Yu-ho, using Hsu’s own 1897 gentry-badge résumé) treats that as an error — he was a *shengyuan*, not a metropolitan graduate.

## Identity Snapshot

- **Salutation:** 許凌雲秀才 / Hsu Ling-yun
- **Chinese:** 許凌雲（名漢厚，字凌雲，號卓夫）
- **Tâi-lô:** Khóo Lîng-hûn
- **Born:** 1862, Tongzhi 1, lunar 6/29, 下瓦硐
- **Died:** 14 Sep 1944 (Shōwa 19; lunar 7/27), age 83; funeral October, attended by Baisha *shōchō* Miyamoto Chikuichi / Lü Zhu (宮本築一／呂築) and Tainan Seminary professor Rev. Kao Chin-sheng (高金聲)
- **Roles:** last Penghu Qing *xiucai*; village *sishu* teacher; Tainan Presbyterian Han teacher (~18 years); Dingshan-ao deputy head (1897, one year)
- **Language:** gazetteer says he never learned Japanese, kept old exam-essay style, was “拙於治生”
- **Geography:** Watong ↔ Tongliang / Houliao / Magong ↔ Tainan ↔ Wufeng / Gangshan

## Timeline

- **Chongzhen (clan)** — Watong Hsu from Jinmen Zhupu (珠浦). Village founders include 許靖奕 (1618–1698), 許靖博, 許靖樂, 許靖六. Singer **Hsu Ching-chun (許景淳)** is recorded as 靖樂 11th-generation — **same village clan, not shown as Ling-yun’s child line**.
- **1862** — Born, lunar 6/29, 下瓦硐. Japanese-era household: 瓦硐港鄉47番戶.
- **1880 (GX6)** — Penghu *ting* exam, rank 32 (age 19). Then Tongliang with Chen Xi-ming.
- **1883 (GX9)** — With elder brother at Ruihe cloth firm (瑞和), Tainan 岩後／內宮後. Gazetteer: poor at the ledger, tore a page, scolded by his brother, **went home to study for the exams**.
- **1884** — Teaching desk in Houliao; Tainan prefectural exam, failed.
- **1885–1892** — Taught Watong, Dachikan, Magong; exams kept failing.
- **1889** — Married Chen Yin (陳吟 / 陳拎), daughter of Zhenhai gentry Chen Liao. Daughter Hsu Yu born the next year.
- **1892 (GX18)** — Accepted Christianity with brother Hsu Han-dun (漢墩／漢敦, 字其仁).
- **1893 (GX19)** — Hsu Yu-ho (from the 1897 résumé): 1st on Penghu *ting* list in month 1; April Tainan, first on Gu Dao-xian’s (顧道憲) *kejia* red list, *rupan* age 32. *白沙鄉志* agrees 1893; *人物志* / Yang still print 1891.
- **1893** — Son Hsu Shui-chin (許水錦) born.
- **1894** — Invited by Yeh Chao-hsien; Magong teaching; part-time Wenshi Academy director. Surviving academy essays (first-place): 〈乘桴浮于海〉 (俞山長: 「文氣清爽機調亦圓」), 〈魯一變〉, 〈為湯武敺民者〉; also 〈求也藝至為費宰〉, 〈蘇武牧羊〉, 〈傳教新語利國益民〉, 〈瑤池戲瑞圖〉.
- **1895** — Shimonoseki; Penghu under Japan.
- **1897 (M30)** — *臺灣紳章*. **11 Jan: Dingshan-ao deputy head** (履歷／許玉河). Gazetteer variant: “赤崁辦務署署長” — treat as a conflicting office title, not a second post.
- **1898** — *Ao* offices abolished; left Penghu for Tainan church schools.
- **1901-03-11** — Campbell, *Handbook of the E.P. Mission in Formosa*: appointed teacher at the Tainan Presbyterian middle school.
- **1904-04-14** — same: “College Teacher.”
- **1909-04-28** — same: girls’ school, Han literature. Pupils thought him deeply learned. ~18 years total. Named Tainan pupils: Lin Yen-chen (林燕臣), Lin Mosei (林茂生).
- **1918 (T7)** — Left seminary post; one year as *xibin* to Lin Lieh-tang (林烈堂), teaching son **Lin Chui-chu (林垂珠)** and the other children.
- **1919 (T8)** — *jishi* (記室 / secretary) to Dr. Kao Tsai-chu (高再祝), Gangshan — gazetteer wording, not only “tutor.”
- **1920** — Home; *Cunyangxuan* (存養軒).
- **1924 (T13)** — Used **Watong Presbyterian church** as the *sishu*. Village Han teaching through 1928 (~8 years). Later village pupils: Ke Jen-yi (柯仁義), Hsu Nan-pu (許南浦), Fang Ssu-wen (方思溫), Lin Yu-chuang (林有闖).
- **1933-10-05** — Lin Hsien-tang and Cai Peihuo, with Wu Qiu-wei (吳秋微), Kao Shun-hsien (高順賢), Hsu Yung-yuan (許永遠), meals at Hsu Chu (許助); call on the already-blind Hsu.
- **1937** — Fourth son Hsu Hsi-chou’s Tainan business ordered closed; he returned to nurse his parents.
- **1941** — Golden wedding with Chen Yin; Hsu Hsi-chou opened **精巧堂** (watches) on Magong Street.
- **1944** — 精巧堂 destroyed in the Penghu air raid. Hsu died 14 Sep; funeral in October.

## Faith works

Conversion path is medical-mission, then school and house-church:

1. **Gao Yao (高耀 / 德耀 / 「蕃仔耀」)** of Tainan First Church — baptized 1869 by Hugh Ritchie; first local Western pharmacist (**仁和堂**). Cured Hsu Han-dun of opium disease; Han-dun converted. Lai Yung-hsiang (*教會史話* 321): Han-dun became **the first Penghu deacon**, Magong Church, **1908**.
2. Gao Yao gave **50 yen** to Campbell’s 1886 Penghu mission drive (half a preacher’s half-year pay). When the mission reached 150 yen, the Mission Council sent Huang Shen-he (黃深河) and seminarian Huang Neng-jie (黃能傑) — Chikan → Jiangmei → Watong.
3. Until the **1911** Watong church, worship borrowed rooms, including **Hsu Han-dun’s house**.
4. Hsu Ling-yun taught Han **inside the church** after 1924. Door couplet still reads gospel + “holy teaching.”
5. Fourth son Hsu Hsi-chou is listed as **elder** (長老, 1913–1980) on Lai’s *本土信徒* card, and as founder of **臺灣雅歌樂器** (Yage / “Song of Songs” instruments).
6. Funeral (1944) included **Rev. Kao Chin-sheng** of Tainan Seminary.

Wikipedia’s *Guangxu* year-numbers next to some Penghu-mission AD dates do not match; prefer Campbell / Lai / Meiji dates above.
