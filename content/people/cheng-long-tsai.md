## Role in the Community

蔡正隆 (1944–1995) was a principal advocate in the 陳文成 (Chen Wen-chen) murder-case campaign. Together with 陳唐山 (Mark Chen) and 陳素貞 (Chen Su-jen) he testified before the U.S. Congress, and their testimonies were entered in Congress' permanent records; the two hearings gave the community confidence in congressional lobbying and gave birth to FAPA ([[works/taiwaneseamericanhistory-org/ourjourneys8-eng|Evan Ling 林富文 memoir]]; [[works/taiwaneseamericanhistory-org/ourjourneys321|許永華 memoir]]:「至於蔡正隆以眞實姓名，眞面目到美國國會作證，更是有種的朋友。」).

Per the ROC black-list roster in [[works/taiwaneseamericanhistory-org/ourjourneys106|何文亮、莊峻華]]: he served as 同鄉會會長 in 1985 and later returned to Taiwan under the alias「蔡巫福」promoting the Taiwan independence movement, was expelled, and was then at Exxon — consistent with the Exxon/engineering-consultant employment table above. The same memoir gives his education as 台大機械系 and a Pittsburgh Carnegie-Mellon engineering Ph.D. (reconciles with the 卡內基大學 1972 Ph.D. line above).

After his death in 1995 the community established the 蔡正隆博士紀念基金會 ([[works/taiwaneseamericanhistory-org/cheng-long-tsai-fund|TAH #50]], record 2018-01-02), and his memorial essay collection《燭火闖關》is held as [[works/taiwaneseamericanhistory-org/262-e7-87-ad-e7-81-ab-e9-97-96-e9-97-9c-e8-94-a1-e6-ad-a3-e9-9a-86-e5-8d-9a-e5-a|TAH #262 / 胡民祥]].
