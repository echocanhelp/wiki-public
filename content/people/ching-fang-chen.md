# Rev. Ching Fang Chen (陳清芳)

**Rev. Ching Fang Chen (陳清芳, Chen Ching Fang)** is a Taiwanese American pastor and the inaugural Chair of the NTPC Historical Committee, appointed in 2025 to lead the preservation and transmission of the National Taiwanese Presbyterian Council's (NTPC) faith history. He authored the foreword to the 2025 republication of Dr. Albert S. Lai's 1971 dissertation *Toward A Community of Hope — A Mission to Formosan Community in Los Angeles*.

## Role in NTPC

### Inaugural Chair, NTPC Historical Committee (2025)

Following the NTPC's 50th anniversary celebrations in 2024 — during which Rev. David Huang (黃德利) edited a commemorative publication chronicling five decades of spiritual journey — the NTPC took a further and significant step: the official establishment of the NTPC Historical Committee in 2025.

Rev. Chen was asked to serve as its inaugural chairperson, an honor he described as "imbued with both solemn responsibility and sacred calling." The Committee's mandate is to preserve and pass on the faith history of NTPC, convening under the theme "Preserving and Passing On the Faith History of NTPC" at the 2025 Annual Conference.

### Foreword to 2025 Republication

Rev. Chen authored the NTPC Historical Committee foreword (dated June 2025), in which he articulated the vision and theological significance of the committee's work. The foreword was included in the 2025 reprint of Dr. Albert S. Lai's 1971 doctoral dissertation, published by Taiwan Church Press and the Incorporated Association of Tainan East Gate Barclay History Education Promotion.

## Foreword Content Summary

Rev. Chen's foreword articulated several key themes:

- **"The history of the Church is a testament to the grace of God"** — a record not only of divine providence but of the fellowship, labor, and witness of God's people through the ages.
- **Urgency of preservation** — "Half a century has passed in the blink of an eye. Many of the pioneering stories and tear-stained testimonies of those who sowed in faith are at risk of fading as the generations who lived them grow old and pass on. Without timely preservation, these precious memories may be lost to time."
- **History as theological encounter** — "History is where we meet God, and it is also the gift one generation entrusts to the next."
- **Cornerstone resource** — Particular gratitude was expressed for Dr. Albert S. Lai's 1971 dissertation, described as "a rare and invaluable document" offering "a detailed and moving account of the founding of the very first Presbyterian Church in Taiwan in Southern California, as well as the missionary vision that inspired its formation."
- **Institutional endorsement** — The NTPC Executive Committee formally endorsed and supported the publication, signaling the Council's affirmation of historical ministry as a vital calling.
- **Call to broader church** — He called on more churches to actively document and preserve their own testimonies of faith, so that "the histories of NTPC churches throughout the nation be seen, heard, understood, and faithfully handed down."

## Legacy & Significance

Rev. Chen's appointment as the first Chair of the NTPC Historical Committee marks a deliberate institutional commitment to memory-keeping within the Taiwanese American Presbyterian community. His foreword frames history not as retrospective nostalgia but as theological foundation — the ground upon which future mission is built.

His leadership signals a transition from the NTPC's immigrant-church era into a second half-century defined by intentional stewardship of its spiritual heritage. The Committee's work, beginning with the republication of Dr. Lai's dissertation, positions the NTPC to honor its pioneers while inspiring new generations to carry forward the mission.
