## Role in the Community

- The corpus history of the Taiwanese American Foundation ([[works/taiwaneseamericanhistory-org/ourjourneys33-2|33. 台美協進會的歷史]]; English version [[works/taiwaneseamericanhistory-org/ourjourneys33-eng|33. History of TAF]]) names Cho a founding member of the Midwest Formosan Christian Foundation (photo of MFCF founders incl. Carol Cho and Bill Cho, circa 1990) and one of the six first-board directors who passed the by-law renaming MFCF to TAF in Chicago on October 18, 1980 — alongside 莊明哲, 張信義, 陳植哲, 洪宏謨, and 楊忠正 — after which TAF launched its first youth programs.
- In retirement he gave back as a teacher in the community's lifelong-learning programs: the Irvine 台灣長老教會 (ITPC) 長青教室 curriculum lists Cho as the lecturer for 「人生下半場」 ([[works/taiwaneseamericanhistory-org/ourjourneys107|107. 長青教室 sponsored by Irvine台灣長老教會(ITPC) / 2015/02]]).
- He also wrote from inside the movement: his own memoir of the 1994 TAF youth summer camp, 「意外的收獲」, is preserved in the corpus ([[works/taiwaneseamericanhistory-org/ourjourneys262|262. 意外的收獲 記1994年TAF青少年夏令營活動 / 卓甫良 /10/2016]]), complementing the 1981 camp memoir written by a camper's parent.
- Name-collision note: the roundtable in the 2024 TAF–Hong Konger community event report [[works/taiwaneseamerican-org/a-tale-of-two-islands-fire-ex-ignite-community|A Tale of Two Islands: Fire-ex Ignite Community]] names a "Pastor William Chou" of the Formosan United Methodist Church — a different person (clergy, not the engineer/nuclear-safety specialist Cho 卓甫良). Not merged.
- The English TAF history names Cho and Dr. James "Jim" Chen as the founders of MFCF, with origins in the mid-1960s wave of Taiwanese graduate students who settled in the Midwest after their studies — HOLD: origins 1966 (History of TAF) vs founding 1973 (楊遠薰); plausibly retreat origins 1966, formal MFCF founding 1973. The same account adds that Cho served as Executive Director of MFCF/TAF from 1973 to 1987, before his fourth presidency ([[works/taiwaneseamericanhistory-org/ourjourneys33-eng|33. History of TAF]]).
- Family involvement in the foundation's first fund-raising program after the October 18, 1980 rename: his second daughter Esther 卓慧文 gave a fund-raising piano recital at DePaul University, Chicago, upon her 18-year high-school graduation, raising $750 for the TAF Fund; wife Carol 林秋菊 is named among the women who launched the Junior/Junior-High art and musical programs under Dr. 張信義's vision ([[works/taiwaneseamericanhistory-org/ourjourneys33-eng|33. History of TAF]]).
- The corpus holds a dedicated biographical record for Carol Cho 林秋菊 alongside Cho's own ([[works/taiwaneseamericanhistory-org/205-carol-cho|205. Carol Cho 林秋菊 / 2015/01]]), confirming the family's standing as a documented pair in the TAH collection.

## Story

William Cho (卓甫良), born in 1932 in Hualien, 台灣花蓮, became one of the Taiwanese graduate students who settled in the American Midwest as an engineer. He left Taiwan by cargo ship in 1956, plighting his engagement to 林秋菊 (Carol) in Tokyo along the way; he then earned a B·S in 結構力學 at the University of Michigan (1959) and an M·S at the Illinois Institute of Technology (1964). His 42-year engineering career ran from Procon (1960) through A·G· Mckee (1964) and Sargent & Laundy (1968), and ended with the State of Illinois nuclear-safety certification expert retiring at the end of 2002. Alongside it he gave 20+ years of servant-leadership to the Midwest Formosan Christian Foundation (MFCF), which grew out of the 1967 lakeside retreat and was formally founded in 1973 — Executive Director from 1973 to 1987, one of the six first-board directors who renamed it the Taiwanese American Foundation (TAF) in Chicago on October 18, 1980, and fourth president from 1987 with an ethics-values theme. He was a founder of the first TAF youth summer camp in 1981 at Calvin College, Michigan, where his daughter Elsa 卓慧麗 co-instructed the Junior Camp, and his second daughter Esther 卓慧文 raised $750 for the TAF Fund with a piano recital at DePaul University. In Irvine he wrote the memoir 「意外的收獲」 of the 1994 TAF youth summer camp and taught 「人生下半場」 in the ITPC 長青教室 listed for 2015/02. The camp memoir records that Cho passed away ("安息主懷") in May 2021, announced in 太平洋時報 2021-06-21, while 楊遠薰's profile 《卓甫良與TAF的故事》 continues to be reprinted and archived as late as 2025.
