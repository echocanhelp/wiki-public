## Filmmaking & Diaspora Record (absorbed from vault corpus)

Writing as **Anita Chang**, she is an independent filmmaker born to parents who immigrated to the U.S. from Taiwan in the 1960s, and grew up in Akron, Ohio and Massachusetts (bio recorded in the vault essay [[works/taiwaneseamerican-org/taiwanese-american-film||Taiwanese American Film — TAH Magazine feature]]). Her 52-minute documentary **62 Years and 6,500 Miles Between** (2005) — in which, amidst political upheaval in Taiwan and the U.S., she navigates cultural, geographical, and linguistic distances in search of wisdom and hope from her 100-year-old Taiwanese activist grandmother (*Ama*) — was showcased in that feature as part of the emerging Taiwanese American film canon, and the same essay credits her with the moment the Center for Asian American Media (formerly NAATA) finally added "Taiwanese" as an ethnicity category in its catalog. Her parents emigrated from Taiwan in the 1960s fleeing a dictatorship (TAH Magazine feature, 2007-05-31). A later Duke senior thesis on Taiwanese American film analyzed *62 Years and 6,500 Miles Between* alongside [[people/jean-cheng||Jean Cheng]]'s *Han Chee*, evidence of her place in the canon.

Her film on leprosy patients in Taiwan and her move to teach at National Dong Hwa University marked a turn toward work made in Taiwan itself; her community-historical record is held as TAH encyclopedia entry [[works/taiwaneseamericanhistory-org/141-anita-wen-shin-chang-e5-bc-b5-e6-96-87-e9-a6-a8||TAH #141 (2014-12-08)]].

## Role in the Community (deepen-x slice 09141300-5, corpus-absorbed)
- Writing as Anita Chang, her first language was Taiwanese (Minnan), lost after learning English — the language-loss regret that drives her later film work, per her own 2014 interview: [[works/taiwaneseamerican-org/a-chat-with-documentary-filmmaker-anita-chang|A Chat with Documentary Filmmaker Anita Chang (2014-05-07, band A)]].
- Beyond *62 Years and 6,500 Miles Between* (2005), the interview credits her with ~a dozen films, most notably **Joyful Life** (2007), pertinent to the Taiwanese community.
- 2014 — Her documentary **Tongues of Heaven** (young indigenous peoples of Taiwan and Hawaii learning endangered heritage languages) had a limited SF/Bay Area theatrical engagement co-presented by TaiwaneseAmerican.org with CAAM and NATWA — Roxie Theater 2014-10-08, New Parkway Theater Oakland 2014-10-14: [[works/taiwaneseamerican-org/anita-chang-tongues-of-heaven|Tongues of Heaven SF/Bay Area screenings (2014-09-28, band A)]]. Community-press co-presentation of her film is direct movement-organizing record, complementing the CAAM "Taiwanese" category anecdote above.
