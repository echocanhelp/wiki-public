# Martha VanDriel (Martha S.H. VanDriel)

**Colonel Martha S.H. VanDriel** (née Hsu, 許和瑞's daughter) is a retired U.S. Army officer who served for over 26 years. She is the **first Taiwanese-American woman to graduate from the U.S. Military Academy at West Point** (1991), graduating in the top 10% of her class. She retired in September 2017 from the Office of the Secretary of Defense.

## Identity Snapshot

- **English:** Martha S.H. VanDriel (née Hsu)
- **Chinese:** 瑪莎·范德瑞爾
- **Born:** South Pasadena, California (daughter of Henry Hsu 許和瑞)
- **Education:** B.S. International Relations and International Strategic History, West Point (1991); M.A. National Security Studies, UNC-Chapel Hill (2000)
- **Military Service:** 26+ years (1991–2017)
- **Rank:** Colonel (retired)
- **Branch:** Military Intelligence; later Cyber Command
- **Spouse:** Michael VanDriel (fellow Army officer)
- **Father:** Henry Hsu (許和瑞), community leader; daughter Martha
- **Geography:** Washington, D.C. / Pentagon / various military assignments

## Early Life

Martha's parents, Henry Hsu and his wife, came to the United States in the early 1960s as graduate students from Taiwan. They met and married in California.

Before Martha was born, her father Henry had written several letters to his family in Taiwan that were critical of the ruling KMT government. The government intercepted the letters, placed Henry on a blacklist, and harassed his family so badly that they told him, "For your safety, do not return to Taiwan. And for our safety, please do not communicate with us any longer." Henry refocused his future on staying in the United States, where he could earn a living, raise his family in safety, and express his political views peacefully.

Martha grew up in South Pasadena, California, where she excelled in high school. As a straight-A student, Academic Decathlon award winner, head cheerleader, editor of the school literary magazine, and swim team member, she was voted "Most Intelligent" and "Most Likely To Succeed" by her high school class.

When looking at potential colleges to attend, many people encouraged her to become a doctor, a lawyer, or an engineer — the usual accepted occupations for many Asian Americans. But Martha said: "I wanted to be different. I didn't want to be typical. I felt that I had something to prove, and I wanted to gain the respect of others."

She was intrigued by West Point and its famous graduates, and by how you couldn't go there just because you did well academically — you had to do well academically, physically, and leadership-wise.

## West Point

Martha's Christian faith led her to accept West Point's offer of admission. She said: "I knew that this is where God wanted me to be, and this helped me to stay at West Point, especially when things were very difficult during my first year there."

The difficult academic, physical, and leadership discipline at West Point scared Martha, because these factors had historically caused about 30% of each West Point freshman class to drop out before graduation. In the end, Martha's Christian faith led her to accept West Point's offer.

In 1991, Martha graduated in the top 10% of her West Point class with a Bachelor of Sciences Degree in International Relations and International Strategic History. She was commissioned as a Second Lieutenant in the Military Intelligence branch of the U.S. Army.

## Military Career

### Early Career

Martha's early career was marked by success. She was the Honor Graduate of her West Point class. Her early assignments included:

- **Company Commander** at U.S. and South Korean Forces-Iraq Headquarters, Al Faw Palace, Camp Victory, Baghdad, in 2010
- Multiple leadership positions in Military Intelligence

### Cyber Command

In 2011, at the rank of Lieutenant Colonel, Martha was appointed the **first Chief of Plans and Policy** of the newly formed **U.S. Army Cyber Command**. This was a critical role in establishing the organizational structure and operational framework for the Army's cyber operations.

### Post-West Point Teaching

After company command, Martha married another Army officer, Michael VanDriel. She was accepted into an Army program that paid for her Master's degree so that she could return to West Point to teach cadets. Therefore, in 2000, Martha earned a **M.A. in National Security Studies from UNC-Chapel Hill**, then returned to West Point to teach International Relations, International Security Studies, and the Politics and Strategy of Cyberwarfare.

### Final Assignments

Martha's last assignment before retiring in September 2017 was at the **Office of the Principal Cyber Advisor** at the Pentagon, where she served as the主管 (director) of the office that coordinates the personnel and training of nearly 30 different cyber warfare units under the U.S. Department of Defense.

Her earlier role was at the **Office of the Secretary of Defense**.

## Retirement

Martha VanDriel retired from the U.S. Army in September 2017 after 26 years of service. She was immediately recruited by **Merck Pharmaceuticals** to serve as the head of their IT risk management department.

## Legacy

Martha VanDriel's story is remarkable for several reasons:
1. She was the **first Taiwanese-American woman** to graduate from West Point
2. She served in one of the most physically and mentally demanding roles in the U.S. military
3. She rose to the rank of Colonel, one of the highest ranks achievable by a career officer
4. She was instrumental in establishing the U.S. Army's cyber warfare capabilities
5. Her father's experience with political persecution in Taiwan shaped the family's values of freedom and democracy
