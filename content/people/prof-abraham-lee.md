## Story

Abraham P Lee 李伯晃教授 was educated at National Tsing Hua University in Power Mechanical Engineering and at UC Berkeley in Mechanical Engineering, with degrees in 1986 and 1992. From 1992 to 1999 he held research posts at Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory, rising to Group Leader, Microstructures in the Center for Microtechnology, and he then led programs as Program Manager of the Microsystems Technology Office at DARPA from 1999 to 2001. He spent 2001 as Senior Technology Advisor at the National Cancer Institute in Bethesda, joined the UC Irvine faculty in 2002 as Professor of Biomedical Engineering, added a joint appointment in Mechanical & Aerospace Engineering in 2003, chaired the BME department from 2010 to 2019, and was named the William J Link Professor of Biomedical Engineering. His research centered on biomedical microfluidics, spanning liquid biopsy, single-cell analysis, and organ-on-a-chip systems, and he directed the DARPA-supported MF3 Center from 2006 to 2013 while editing the journal Lab on a Chip from 2017 to 2020. His inventions enabled the startups Micrus Endovascular Corp, acquired by Johnson & Johnson in 2010, and SFC Fluidics, and honors included fellow of the National Academy of Inventors in 2018 and of the International Academy of Medical and Biological Engineering in 2022. In 2025 he returned to his alma mater as 玉山學者 and adjunct faculty at National Tsing Hua University, his mother 劉惠麗 wrote in her memoirs that her eldest son serves at Irvine, and T.A. Archives honored him as an Outstanding Taiwanese American in 2019.
