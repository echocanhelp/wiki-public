# Dr. Shoki Coe (黃彰輝)

**Dr. Shoki Coe (黃彰輝)** is a prominent Taiwanese-American theologian and ecumenical leader whose work bridged Taiwan's church history with global theological discourse.

## Identity Snapshot
- Era: 20th–21st century theological influence
- Geography: Taiwan and global ecumenical movement
- Core roles: theologian, ecumenical leader, church historian

## Historical Context
Dr. Coe is recognized as a key figure in Taiwan's Christian ecumenical movement and has contributed significantly to contextual theology and cross-cultural church dialogue.

## Contributions and Legacy
1. Pioneering work in Taiwanese ecumenical cooperation
2. Bridging Taiwanese church history with global theological discourse
3. Influence on diaspora identity and mission debates

## Vault records (deepen pass 2026-09-10)
From [[works/taiwaneseamericanhistory-org/our-journeys-350|張瑞雄牧師口述]] and the TAH biography record [[works/taiwaneseamericanhistory-org/601-e5-8f-b0-e7-81-a3-e4-ba-ba-e7-9a-84-e5-85-88-e8-a6-ba-e9-bb-83-e5-bd-b0-e8-b|TAH #601《台灣人的先覺者：黃彰輝》(張瑞雄, 2004)]]:
- Born 1914 in Changhua; transferred from Tainan Presbyterian Middle School to Gakushuin (青山學院) senior year; then Taipei Higher School, Tokyo Imperial University, and study in Britain (lodged with the family of Dr. Ran, a physician-missionary to Taiwan; taught Japanese to British military personnel; 426 related files later found at SOAS).
- Postwar returned to Taiwan as President of Tainan Theological College (台南神學院院長); trained [[people/kao-chun-ming|高俊明 Kao Chun-ming]]. Classmate of [[people/rev-ai-chih-tsai|蔡愛智 Rev. Ai-chih Tsai]], who warned him not to return after 228.
- 1965: appointed General Secretary of the World Council of Theological Education (世界普世神學教育基金會), leaving Taiwan while continuing to support the island's democracy and human rights movements.
- Died 1988; his English memoir was published in 1991; the biography 台灣人的先覺 by Zhang Ruishiou (who studied under Coe at Tainan Theological College) was completed in 2003 (TAH #601, 2004).
