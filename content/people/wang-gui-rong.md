# Wang Gui-rong (王桂榮)

**Wang Gui-rong** (1931–2012), also known in English as **Kenjohn Wang**, was a Taiwanese American entrepreneur, philanthropist, and community leader. He built a hotel and real estate empire in Southern California, founded the Taiwanese American Foundation (TAF), and served in multiple advisory roles to the governments of Taiwan and the United States. He is widely regarded as one of the most generous benefactors in the Taiwanese American community.

## Early Life

Wang Gui-rong was born in 1931 in Taiwan, then under Japanese colonial rule. He attended the Junior College of Administration of Taiwan (now part of National Taipei University) and graduated in 1951. He then joined the military, serving as a lieutenant translator for the U.S. Military Assistance Advisory Group (MAAG) at the Tainan air base from 1955–1956 — a role that established his first connections with Americans.

His future wife, 王賽美 (Jacqueline Wang), was studying at 靜宜英專 (now 靜宜 University, then a junior college in Taichung) and worked at the Taipei American School library and as an elementary school teacher. They married and built their early lives around Taiwan's English-language and military-adjacent institutions.

Wang originally continued his family's international trade, Western medicine manufacturing, and import-export businesses in Taiwan. In 1965, he began graduate studies in politics at Kansas State University, then transferred to the University of Texas at Austin for a PhD. Realizing the practical demands of supporting a family, he switched to accounting and eventually opened a CPA firm in Washington, D.C.

## Immigration to the United States

Wang's decision to immigrate was driven by geopolitical fear. In July 1971, Henry Kissinger secretly visited Beijing on behalf of President Nixon. In October 1971, the UN expelled the Chiang Kai-shek government's representative. Wang recalled: "Friends told each other [that the Chiang regime was expelled from the UN] and all believed Taiwan would fall into chaos." His fear that Taiwan might fall under Chinese Communist rule was shared by most Taiwanese residents at the time.

Wang and his wife initially planned to immigrate to Paraguay. In 1972, one year after the Shanghai Communique, they migrated to Paraguay. However, while transiting through the United States on their way to South America, they stopped in California for half a year. Despite having no legal status at the time, they enrolled their three children in a nearby elementary school. The principal, moved by compassion, allowed the children to attend and even provided school bus transportation, free lunches, and a dedicated English teacher. Wang later described this experience as "imagine something so wonderful happening in a foreign land — I suddenly wondered if America was the paradise described in Buddhist scriptures."

This experience established Wang's deep affection for America and resolved his commitment to give back. He later wrote: "I resolved to earn money, pay taxes, earn more, and pay more taxes, to repay this warm society."

They ultimately abandoned their Paraguay plan and applied for immigration to the United States — a decision Wang regarded as one of the most significant and correct choices of his life.

## Hotel Empire

After settling in California, Wang began his business career in the hospitality industry. In 1974, he purchased his first hotel, the **Newland Apartment Motel** in Westminster, Orange County. The family worked together — Wang described it as "sweat and toil" (胼手胝足) — and the first year's profits far exceeded expectations.

In 1975, Wang purchased the long-neglected **Outrigger Inn** in Long Beach, California. He invested heavily in renovations and operations, transforming the property completely. This hotel became the cornerstone of his empire: he joined the **Ramada Inn** chain, and Wang later refused lucrative buyout offers because of his special attachment to the property. He expanded it into a mid-to-large hotel, with the ground floor as commercial space and the upper floors housing the Wang family enterprise headquarters.

His hotel empire grew to include:
- **Ramada Inn / Outrigger Inn** (Long Beach) — the flagship
- **Holiday Inn** (Montebello, purchased 1978-07)
- **Atrium Hotel**
- Additional properties in Southern California

Wang earned the nickname "旅館大王" (Hotel King) among Taiwanese American business circles. During the 1973–1978 period, when Taiwan's international situation deteriorated and the U.S. economy was in recession, many Taiwanese business leaders and military officials' children came to Southern California seeking guidance. Wang welcomed all of them, shared his business secrets freely, and became known as a generous mentor.

## Philanthropy and Community Leadership

Wang's philanthropy was extraordinary in scale and scope. He focused on two areas: giving back to his new country (America) and to his homeland (Taiwan).

### Taiwanese American Foundation (TAF)

In 1982, Wang donated **$1 million** to establish the **Taiwanese American Foundation (TAF)**, which earned him the title "The Nobel of Taiwan" (台灣的諾貝爾). TAF was created to honor outstanding individuals who "care about Taiwan and identify Taiwan as their homeland." From 1983 onward, laureates included 廖述宗, 柏楊, 李登輝, 彭明敏, 李遠哲, 蘭大弼, and many others from around the world.

### LA Taiwan Center

In 1988, Wang donated his property to establish the **Los Angeles Taiwan Center** (大洛杉磯台灣會館) in Rosemead, California. His vision was: "I always had a wish that Los Angeles would have a Taiwan Center as a home for Taiwanese organizations, a spiritual fortress to promote our culture, bring it into mainstream American society, give back to American society, and raise the status of Taiwanese people in America."

### USC Scholarship

In 1997, he established the **Kenjohn Wang Scholarship** at the University of Southern California (USC).

### Other Contributions

- Founded the **Taiwan Hotel and Motel Association of Southern California** (1974)
- Founded the **Taiwanese Chambers of Commerce of North America** (1987)
- Funded and published a commerce-oriented Taiwanese American community newspaper (1980)
- Appointed to the Finance Committee by DNC Chairman Ronald H. Brown (1989)
- President of FAPA (Formosan Association for Public Affairs) (1989)
- Decade Award from the White House (via Asia-Pacific American Heritage Committee)
- Member of the President's Advisory Board under President Chen Shui-bian (2004–2005)

## Political Advocacy

Wang was deeply involved in Taiwanese American political organizing. He served as:
- President of **FAPA** (Formosan Association for Public Affairs), a lobby arm for Taiwanese Americans
- Member of the Finance Committee, Democratic National Committee (1989)
- Appointed to the President's Advisory Board by President Chen Shui-bian (2004–2005)
- Helped organize Taiwanese American political participation in U.S. electoral processes

He understood "the rules of the game" in American society and believed that political engagement was essential to elevating the status of Taiwanese Americans in mainstream society.

## Death and Legacy

Wang Gui-rong died on 2012-02-11 at Taipei Medical University Hospital in Taipei, where he was receiving medical treatment. At the time of his death, he was still a U.S. citizen. He was 81–82 years old.

He left behind two books, including his memoirs published in 1999. His three sons — Kenneth, John, and Michael — now manage the Kenjohn Group, which includes his hotel businesses, real estate investments, and trade operations in both America and Taiwan.
