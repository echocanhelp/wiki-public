## Record

Ten-part documentary series tracing Taiwan's story across four centuries — Indigenous peoples through successive waves of migration, colonization, conflict, and democratization. Created/written/directed by Jay Liao; editing Jay Liao + Sam Martin. YouTube trailer/watch anchor: `youtube.com/watch?v=cZXYh0X48fU`.

### Episode guide (site synopses, archived)

1. **Austronesian origins** — earliest inhabitants, migration patterns, social structures, cultural foundations before sustained external contact
2. **European contest, 17th c.** — Dutch and Spanish footholds, trade, missionaries, Indigenous resistance
3. **1661 — Zheng Chenggong (Koxinga)** — siege of Fort Zeelandia, end of Dutch rule, new regime
4. **Qing frontier, late 17th c.–1895** — migration settlement, land disputes, Indigenous resistance, periodic unrest
5. **Japanese colony 1895–1945** — modernization alongside policing, assimilation, local resistance
6. **White Terror, late 1940s–1980s** — martial law, arrests, military tribunals, surveillance of civil society
7. **1949 — two governments** — ROC in Taiwan vs PRC mainland; Chiang Kai-shek to Chiang Ching-kuo
8. **Democratization, late 1980s onward** — end of martial law, opposition movements, expanding civil participation
9. **The Silicon Shield** — semiconductor centrality in global supply chains and its strategic weight
10. **Present day** — supply chains, regional security, geopolitical tension, debates over political status and identity
